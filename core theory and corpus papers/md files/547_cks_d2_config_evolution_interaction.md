## Abstract

Claim 4 of Paper 3 establishes the four-locus evolution-feed mechanism operating at the Full Aspect Integration (FAI) hand-off boundary. Claim 5 of Paper 3 establishes that every dimension of the FAI protocol is configuration expressed as substrate content, authored under human governance. This note formalizes D2.52 as the inter-claim operational synthesis of these two claims: the evolution feed is not an automatic transfer from shared substrate to home evolution machinery, but a governed, configured mechanism. Five evolution feed configuration objects are identified — action-feedback eligibility scope, DNA evolution eligibility scope, hand-off boundary configuration, provenance carry-over depth, and provenance preservation on internalization — each of which is authored governance content and each of which governs a distinct decision point in the evolution feed pathway. The note states the prior-art significance of this synthesis, the amendment effects on the evolution feed during an active event, and the anti-pattern of ungoverned evolution feed that the architecture forecloses. An operational test is provided.

## 1. D2.52 as Inter-Claim Operational Synthesis

D2.52 formalizes the operational intersection of two claims that Paper 3 establishes in sequence but whose interaction requires explicit articulation.

Claim 4 specifies the four-locus evolution-feed mechanism: when a shared substrate dissolves at the close of a FAI event, content propagates to each participating Self's home substrate through a governed ingestion process. The four loci are (a) the action-feedback locus, where FAI event outcomes and records feed each Self's home action layer; (b) the DNA evolution locus, where shared-substrate DNA-layer content is available for absorption consideration by each Self's home governance; (c) a layer-routing rule that routes DNA-layer content to DNA evolution and action-layer content to action-feedback evolution while excluding instinct-layer content from cross-Self exchange by architectural commitment; and (d) asymmetric ingestion, where what each participating Self takes from the dissolution is determined by its own home governance rather than by a uniform distribution rule.

Claim 5 specifies that every dimension of the FAI protocol is configuration expressed as substrate content, with recursive applicability. The recursion bottoms out at human-authored governance authority: humans hold authority over configuration substrate; the architecture supports LLM participation in drafting and populating configuration substrate as substrate mediators, but does not permit configuration to be authored outside human authority. The seventeen cumulative configurable dimensions that §§5–7 of Paper 3 develop collectively land at Claim 5's scope as substrate content authored under joint authority across participating Selves' governance.

The inter-claim synthesis is this: the Claim 4 evolution-feed mechanism is not merely governed at the hand-off boundary — it is governed throughout, at every decision point where a channel from shared substrate to home evolution machinery could exist. Claim 5 governs Claim 4 by making each of those channels a configured object. What flows, from where, to whom, carrying what provenance, and preserved to what depth on arrival — every one of these is a governance decision expressed as substrate content. The five evolution feed configuration objects named in this note are the specific instantiation of that synthesis.

## 2. Five Evolution Feed Configuration Objects

### Object 1 — Action-Feedback Eligibility Scope

What governs: which FAI event outcomes and records are eligible for ingestion into each participating Self's home action layer at dissolution.

Not every FAI event record needs to enter every participating Self's action-feedback evolution machinery. Which event records are in scope for ingestion is an authored governance decision. Governance may scope this narrowly — only records pertaining to a Self's own contributed aspects — or broadly, to include the full action-layer content that accumulated during the FAI event. It may define eligibility by content class, by event phase, by conflict status, or by other criteria the governing parties choose to author. What the architecture requires is that the eligibility scope be present as authored governance content and that what actually enters the action-feedback locus at dissolution matches it.

This is Claim 5 governing Claim 4 Locus (a). Without this configuration object, the architecture has no mechanism for a Self's home governance to authorize or restrict which event records feed its action-feedback evolution.

### Object 2 — DNA Evolution Eligibility Scope

What governs: which shared-substrate DNA-layer content is eligible for absorption consideration by each participating Self's home governance.

DNA-layer content contributed by one Self during a FAI event may contain orchestration patterns, schemas, or decision-logic structures that another Self's home governance might choose to absorb. Which of this content enters the consideration set for each Self's DNA evolution is a configured eligibility question. The eligibility scope is jointly determined by the sharing-scope configuration (Claim 5 Dimension 1 — which aspects each Self contributes to the shared substrate) and the hand-off boundary configuration (Object 3, below). A Self's home governance then applies its authority architecture to what falls within the eligibility scope, deciding what to absorb, what to decline, and what to hold under review.

This is Claim 5 governing Claim 4 Locus (b). The eligibility scope is not the absorption decision itself — that remains with home governance — but the configured boundary that defines what home governance is considering when it exercises that decision.

### Object 3 — Hand-Off Boundary Configuration

What governs: which content flows to which home substrate at dissolution, along which channel.

The hand-off boundary is the object that specifies the structure of dissolution itself. When the shared substrate dissolves at the close of a FAI event, content does not redistribute automatically. The hand-off boundary configuration specifies the channel: from the shared substrate to each home substrate, per content class, per locus. It is jointly authorized governance content — because dissolution involves multiple participating Selves' home perimeters simultaneously, the hand-off boundary configuration is authored under joint authority across those governance perimeters.

The hand-off boundary configuration is Claim 5 governing the structural frame of Claim 4 as a whole. It is the configuration object that makes the dissolution event a governed transition rather than an automatic transfer. Without it, the minimum viable governance floor for FAI dissolution cannot be met: a hand-off boundary configuration must be present for dissolution to produce governed evolution feed outputs at all.

### Object 4 — Provenance Carry-Over Depth

What governs: how much provenance travels with contributed aspects at the time they enter the shared substrate.

When a Self contributes an aspect to the shared substrate during a FAI event, governance specifies the provenance depth that travels with the contribution. Options range from none — where bare content suffices and authoring history is not task-relevant — to full, where the complete provenance chain travels with the aspect because it carries information the other participating Selves will need when evaluating the content for potential absorption. Intermediate configurations between these poles are expressly within scope.

Object 4 governs the provenance richness of content available at all four Claim 4 evolution-feed loci at dissolution time. It operates at the contribution boundary — the moment an aspect moves from a Self's home substrate into the shared substrate. At that moment, governance has already configured what provenance richness will travel with it. The content that arrives at the evolution-feed loci at dissolution carries whatever provenance depth was configured at contribution time.

This is Claim 5 governing the provenance dimension of all four Claim 4 loci. It is the inward-facing provenance governance decision: what provenance depth enters the shared substrate.

### Object 5 — Provenance Preservation on Internalization

What governs: how much provenance is retained when each Self's home substrate ingests evolution feed outputs.

Object 5 is the outward-facing provenance governance decision: what provenance depth is preserved as evolution feed outputs flow from the shared substrate into each Self's home substrate at dissolution. Even if Object 4 has configured rich provenance to travel with contributed aspects into the shared substrate, each Self's home governance independently decides how much of that provenance to preserve on internalization. A Self may choose to ingest content with full provenance preserved — maintaining long-term audit depth for inter-Self learning. It may choose to ingest content with reduced provenance — retaining only attribution and content class. The decision is home governance content, authored at each participating Self's home perimeter.

Objects 4 and 5 are distinct configured governance decisions operating at different lifecycle moments. Object 4 governs the inward transition at contribution time; Object 5 governs the outward transition at ingestion time. Together they define the provenance architecture of the entire evolution feed pathway, from initial contribution through shared-substrate residence through home-substrate internalization. Both are Claim 5 governing the provenance dimension of Claim 4 — but at different moments and under different governance authority.

## 3. Amendment Effects on the Evolution Feed

Any of the five configuration objects may be amended during an active FAI event. The amendment effects on the evolution feed are direct.

If the action-feedback eligibility scope (Object 1) is amended mid-event — for instance, expanding it to include a content class that was initially excluded — the amended scope governs what is ingested at dissolution. The evolution feed delivers what the configuration says at dissolution time, not at event-open time. Similarly, if the DNA evolution eligibility scope (Object 2) is narrowed mid-event to exclude a category of DNA-layer content, that narrowing takes effect at dissolution regardless of what content accumulated in the shared substrate prior to the amendment.

If the hand-off boundary configuration (Object 3) is amended, the amended channel structure governs dissolution. If provenance carry-over depth (Object 4) is amended after some aspects have already been contributed, the amended depth applies to subsequently contributed aspects; already-contributed content carries the provenance depth that was configured at the time of contribution. If provenance preservation policy (Object 5) is amended, the amended policy governs what each home substrate retains when ingesting dissolution outputs.

Amendment records — authored as substrate content per the architecture's amendment-tracking commitment — track changes to all five configuration objects during an event. This preserves the audit chain: an observer with access to amendment records can reconstruct the configured state of the evolution feed at any moment during the event and verify that what was delivered at dissolution matched the configured state at that time.

## 4. Evolution Feed Completeness Requires Configuration

The Claim 4 evolution feed cannot operate as a governed mechanism without its Claim 5 configuration. This is not a design preference but an architectural entailment. A dissolution event that produces ungoverned outputs — where content reaches home evolution machinery without an authorizing eligibility scope and hand-off boundary configuration — does not instantiate the architecture. It is a different architecture.

The minimum viable governance floor for a FAI event dissolution requires at least a hand-off boundary configuration to be present as authored governance content. Without it, there is no governed channel from the shared substrate to each participating Self's home evolution machinery. The dissolution event either produces no evolution feed outputs, or it produces outputs outside any governed channel — the latter being the anti-pattern named in Section 5.

The five configuration objects are not additive features on top of a functioning evolution feed. They are constitutive of the governed character of the feed. The action-feedback eligibility scope is what makes action-layer ingestion governed. The DNA evolution eligibility scope is what makes DNA-layer absorption consideration governed. The hand-off boundary configuration is what makes dissolution itself governed. The provenance dimensions are what make the audit chain of inter-Self learning governed. Remove any one of them and the corresponding dimension of the evolution feed loses its governed character.

## 5. Prior-Art Significance

The prior-art significance of D2.52 is the establishment of governed, configured evolution feed as a committed architectural property of inter-Self AI coordination operating under the CKS architecture.

Contemporary inter-AI coordination mechanisms — federated learning, multi-agent systems, distributed learning architectures — produce outputs that update participating agents' behavior or parameters through mechanisms that operate without per-channel eligibility governance. Federated learning clients receive updated global model parameters through aggregation; the channel and eligibility are fixed by the protocol. Multi-agent architectures update agent behavior through shared reward signals or communication channels without governed eligibility scope. The asymmetry-from-heterogeneity in federated continual learning and the update-from-protocol-completion in multi-party computation both commit to automatic or semi-automatic update channels.

The CKS architecture commits differently. The five configuration objects establish that what enters each home evolution machinery from inter-Self coordination is a governance decision at every decision point. There is no automatic channel. Every locus of the evolution feed is configured. Every eligibility scope is authored. Every provenance depth is specified. The evolution feed mechanism operates under the architecture only when all five configuration objects are present as authored governance content.

This forecloses adversarial claims that the evolution-feed mechanism described in Claim 4 is equivalent to existing ungoverned or semi-governed inter-AI update mechanisms. The distinction is architectural, not implementational: the CKS architecture requires configured, authorized evolution feed; the alternative mechanisms do not.

## 6. Anti-Pattern: Ungoverned Evolution Feed

The anti-pattern this note forecloses is ungoverned evolution feed: FAI event outputs flowing to participating Selves' home evolution machinery without configured eligibility scope or hand-off governance. In this anti-pattern, content reaches home action-feedback evolution or home DNA evolution machinery based on system defaults rather than governance decisions. No action-feedback eligibility scope authorizes the ingestion. No DNA evolution eligibility scope bounds the absorption consideration. No hand-off boundary configuration specifies the channel. Provenance depth at contribution and internalization is whatever the system defaults to. The evolution machinery receives inputs it was not governed to receive.

The ungoverned evolution feed anti-pattern is the primary adversarial target of the Claim 5 / Claim 4 synthesis. Any inter-AI coordination mechanism that automatically updates participating AIs' behavior based on coordination outcomes — without governed eligibility scope and home governance authorization at each decision point — instantiates this anti-pattern. The architecture does not permit this by design: the five configuration objects are constitutive of what makes the evolution feed a governed mechanism, and their absence removes the governed character of the feed.

## 7. Operational Test

For a completed FAI event dissolution, the following test verifies that the evolution feed was governed:

1. Can an observer find all five configuration objects as authored governance content within the substrate record for this event? Specifically: (a) action-feedback eligibility scope; (b) DNA evolution eligibility scope; (c) hand-off boundary configuration; (d) provenance carry-over depth specification; (e) provenance preservation policy for each participating Self?

2. Does what entered each participating Self's home action-feedback evolution locus match the action-feedback eligibility scope as configured at dissolution time (accounting for any amendments tracked in the amendment record)?

3. Does the DNA-layer content that entered each participating Self's home governance consideration set for absorption fall within the DNA evolution eligibility scope as configured at dissolution time?

4. Does the provenance depth attached to evolution feed outputs at dissolution match what Object 4 configured at contribution time and what Object 5 configured as the preservation policy on ingestion?

5. Is the channel through which evolution feed outputs reached each home substrate the channel specified by the hand-off boundary configuration?

A FAI event dissolution that passes all five sub-tests is one whose evolution feed was governed in the CKS sense. A dissolution that fails any sub-test either instantiates the ungoverned evolution feed anti-pattern or contains a defect in the amendment record such that the governance state at dissolution cannot be reconstructed.

## 8. Conclusion

D2.52 establishes the operational intersection of Claim 5 (configuration as substrate content) and Claim 4 (four-locus evolution-feed mechanism at the FAI hand-off boundary) as a committed architectural property: the evolution feed is a governed, configured mechanism. Five evolution feed configuration objects compose this commitment — action-feedback eligibility scope, DNA evolution eligibility scope, hand-off boundary configuration, provenance carry-over depth, and provenance preservation on internalization. Each is authored governance content. Each governs a distinct decision point in the pathway from shared substrate to home evolution machinery. Together they establish that no content reaches home evolution machinery from inter-Self coordination without a governing eligibility decision expressed as substrate content.

The amendment effects on the evolution feed preserve the governed character through mid-event configuration changes. The minimum viable governance floor requires at minimum a hand-off boundary configuration to be present. The prior-art claim this synthesis establishes is the architectural requirement for configured, authorized evolution feed in inter-Self AI coordination — a requirement that forecloses equivalence to ungoverned or semi-governed inter-AI update mechanisms in contemporary federated and multi-agent architectures.
