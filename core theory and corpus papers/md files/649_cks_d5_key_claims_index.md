## 2. Phase D5's affirmative contribution to the prior-art corpus

Phase D5 occupies a specific position in the derivation-note series. Phase D2 established what the governance requirements of Paper 3's architecture are — the operational variants and sub-commitments that follow from Paper 3's six named claims. Phases D3 and D4 established failure modes and composition pairs. Phase D5 establishes something different: that the requirements already in the record are **testable and measurable**.

This distinction matters for the prior-art record in two ways. First, a prior-art corpus of vague commitments is vulnerable to the adversarial argument that it names aspirations rather than architectural properties. Binary testability forecloses that argument: if a governance commitment can be verified or falsified from governance records alone, it is not vague. Second, measurability extends the prior-art coverage from static architecture into governance-quality monitoring — an active, ongoing dimension of any deployed system. Phase D5 claims span both of these contributions.

## 3. The ten key prior-art claims

### Claim D5-01: Operational precision of Paper 3's governance commitments

**Statement:** The 60-test library establishes that Paper 3's governance commitments are operationally precise enough to be binary-tested from governance records alone, foreclosing the "vague prior art" adversarial argument.

**Why this is the meta-claim:** D5-01 is both the logical foundation of Phase D5 and the summary of its contribution. If this claim were false — if Paper 3's commitments were too vague to binary-test — Claims D5-02 through D5-10 would be incoherent. The existence of 60 tests that satisfy three validation criteria (binary from records, distinct coverage, non-trivial pass/fail) is the proof that the claim holds. Every more specific claim that follows is an instance of this general property.

**Established by:** D5.07 (complete 60-test library), D5.13 (Phase D5 synthesis).

---

### Claim D5-02: Pre-authorization as the binary-testable solution to the speed-governance tension

**Statement:** Pre-authorization is the binary-testable solution to the speed-governance tension — time-sensitive full-aspect integration events can be governed by verifying that pre-authorized standing configurations were used, not by requiring real-time governance authoring.

**Significance:** The speed-governance tension is a structurally recurring objection to any governance framework applied to time-sensitive coordination: if governance requires human authoring at event time, it will be bypassed under time pressure. Pre-authorization resolves the tension architecturally. Binary testability is what makes the resolution usable as prior art: an assessor can verify from governance records whether standing configurations were pre-authorized and whether the event used them.

**Established by:** D5.02 (Test 21).

---

### Claim D5-03: Binary testability of N-ary governance completeness

**Statement:** N-ary governance completeness is binary-testable — for N greater than two participants in a full-aspect integration event, the presence of three-way joint authorization and cross-pair conflict routing can be verified from governance records.

**Significance:** Paper 3's governance architecture is specified for the two-party case but extends to N participants. This claim establishes that the extension is not merely theoretical: the presence of joint authorization and conflict-routing completeness for all pairs is verifiable. This forecloses a narrow reading that limits the prior-art coverage to two-participant scenarios.

**Established by:** D5.02 (Test 22).

---

### Claim D5-04: Binary testability of the competition variant with preserve-tier routing

**Statement:** The competition variant combined with preserve-tier routing is binary-testable — the presence of both competition variant selection and preserve-tier routing rules can be verified from governance records, detecting misrouting of preserve-tier content.

**Significance:** Paper 3 defines three full-aspect integration pattern variants (collaborative, competitive, complementary) and a three-tier conflict-handling mechanism (preserve, resolve, escalate). The interaction between the competition variant and the preserve tier is a specific failure-adjacent configuration requiring explicit governance attention. Binary testability of both together provides targeted detection of the anti-pattern in which preserve-tier content is misrouted under competitive selection conditions.

**Established by:** D5.02 (Test 23).

---

### Claim D5-05: Governance quality is quantitatively measurable

**Statement:** Governance quality is measurable through test pass rates, trackable over time, and integrable with health monitoring — governance quality has a quantitative dimension.

**Significance:** Most governance frameworks produce binary assessments (compliant or non-compliant at a point in time). This claim extends the prior art into the quantitative dimension: governance quality can be expressed as a pass rate across the test library, tracked as a time series, and integrated with health indicators to create a governance quality assurance cycle. This dimension of the prior-art coverage is novel relative to the binary-compliance framing that dominates governance literature.

**Established by:** D5.08 (calibration guidance), D5.12 (health integration).

---

### Claim D5-06: Six scenario-specific test subsets for practical deployment

**Statement:** Six scenario-specific test subsets make the 60-test library practically usable across different governance contexts without requiring all 60 tests for every event.

**Significance:** A 60-test library applied uniformly to every governance event would be operationally impractical in most deployment contexts. The six scenario-specific subsets — organized around governance audit, event readiness, population assessment, evolution tracking, conflict assessment, and cross-paper compliance — make the library selectively applicable. This claim is prior art for the pattern of subset-structured test libraries, distinct from a claim about any individual test.

**Established by:** D5.09 (scenario-specific subsets).

---

### Claim D5-07: Binary testability of cross-paper lifecycle and evolution isolation

**Statement:** Cross-paper lifecycle and evolution isolation are binary-testable — the version-fixing principle established in earlier derivation notes produces verifiable records distinguishing shared-substrate versions from home-governance-updated versions.

**Significance:** Paper 3 inherits from Papers 1 and 2, and Paper 3's commitments interact with commitments from prior phases. The version-fixing principle (that a Self's shared-substrate version is fixed at participation time and does not propagate home-governance evolution) is a cross-paper architectural claim. This claim establishes that the isolation it describes is verifiable, closing a gap where cross-paper claims might otherwise be viewed as unverifiable assertions.

**Established by:** D5.10 (Tests 56–57).

---

### Claim D5-08: Paper 2 compliance as a binary-testable prerequisite for Paper 3 participation

**Statement:** Paper 2 compliance is a binary-testable prerequisite for full-aspect integration — the contributing party's Paper 2-compliant architecture can be verified as a pre-participation governance check.

**Significance:** Paper 3 builds on Paper 2: a Self participating in full-aspect integration must already instantiate the architectural commitments of Paper 2. This claim establishes that the prerequisite is not just a logical dependency but a verifiable governance check. A governance assessor can verify Paper 2 compliance before allowing participation, creating an enforceable entry condition. This is prior art for the pattern of prerequisite-as-governance-check, which is a reusable pattern across multi-paper architectural frameworks.

**Established by:** D5.10 (Test 60).

---

### Claim D5-09: Health indicators and operational tests as complementary systems

**Statement:** Health indicators and operational tests are complementary systems — health indicators provide continuous monitoring while operational tests provide periodic deep assessment, and their integration creates a unified governance quality assurance cycle.

**Significance:** This claim articulates a structural relationship between two distinct governance tools. Health indicators (from Paper 3's monitoring architecture) are continuously updated signals; operational tests are periodic, deep-assessment instruments. The claim is that they are not alternatives but complements, and that their integration produces a governance quality assurance cycle more complete than either provides alone. This is prior art for the two-layer governance monitoring pattern.

**Established by:** D5.12 (health integration note).

---

### Claim D5-10: Validation honesty as a governance-quality claim about the prior-art corpus

**Statement:** The test library's three validation criteria — binary verifiability from records, distinct coverage across commitments, and non-trivial pass/fail conditions — combined with explicit acknowledgment of gaps, demonstrate intellectual honesty that strengthens the prior-art position.

**Why this claim is distinctive:** Claims D5-01 through D5-09 make affirmative claims about what Paper 3's architecture is and how it can be tested. Claim D5-10 is a claim about the prior-art corpus itself. It asserts that the Phase D5 notes demonstrate intellectual honesty — by stating validation criteria explicitly, by applying those criteria consistently, and by acknowledging what the test library does not cover — and that this self-assessment property strengthens the corpus's credibility.

This matters for the defensive-publication purpose. A prior-art corpus assembled purely for coverage breadth, without explicit quality criteria and acknowledged limitations, is more vulnerable to challenge than one that documents its own assessment standards. Claim D5-10 is the claim that Phase D5 meets this higher standard.

**Established by:** D5.11 (validation criteria and acknowledged gaps).

## 4. Claims structure: meta-claim and instances

The ten claims above have a logical structure worth making explicit.

Claim D5-01 is the meta-claim. It asserts that binary testability from governance records is a property of Paper 3's governance commitments as a class. Claims D5-02, D5-03, D5-04, D5-07, and D5-08 are specific instances of this property, each demonstrating binary testability for a particular commitment or combination of commitments (speed-governance solution, N-ary completeness, competition-pair interaction, cross-paper isolation, and Paper 2 prerequisite, respectively).

Claims D5-05 and D5-06 extend beyond binary testability into the practical and quantitative dimensions: D5-05 asserts that governance quality is measurable as a continuous quantity, and D5-06 asserts that the test library is practically deployable across different contexts through scenario-specific subsets.

Claim D5-09 addresses the architectural relationship between two monitoring systems, positioning tests and health indicators as complementary rather than substitutable.

Claim D5-10 stands apart from the others: it is not a claim about Paper 3's architecture but about the prior-art corpus itself. Its function is to document the quality standard the Phase D5 notes were held to, which in turn supports the credibility of all ten claims.

## 5. Relationship to Phase D2 claims and Phase D3/D4 claims

Phase D2 established what the governance requirements of Paper 3's architecture are. Phase D5 claims do not restate those requirements; they establish that the requirements are testable. The relationship is additive: Phase D5 claims presuppose Phase D2's requirements as their subject matter and add testability as a new property of each.

Phase D3 established failure modes — the anti-patterns that arise when Paper 3's commitments are violated. Phase D4 established composition pairs — the interactions between Paper 3's commitments and commitments from Papers 1 and 2. Phase D5 Tests 56–60, which underlie Claims D5-07 and D5-08, explicitly test for cross-paper compliance and isolation. These tests connect the Phase D5 prior-art chain back to Phase D3 and D4 claims, providing a testability layer over the failure-mode and composition-pair coverage.
