## Abstract

This note is the T5 subseries synthesis — derivation note #714, the twenty-ninth and final substantive note of Series T. It consolidates what T5.01 through T5.04 established about population-scale and topology-related concept disambiguation across the CKS theory trilogy. The synthesis identifies two distinct patterns that emerged from the four T5 notes: a small category of *genuinely new governance concepts* introduced by Paper 3 (trust calibration; governance capacity ceiling), and a larger category of *calibrated-humility projections* — concepts describing emergent dynamics that follow from widespread FAI adoption but that are not governed mechanisms. The central T5 finding is a register distinction: population-scale concepts in the trilogy are not uniformly architectural commitments. Correctly classifying which concepts are governed properties and which are projected emergent consequences is load-bearing for the prior-art position. This note states the two-pattern finding, records the register distinction, summarizes all four T5 disambiguations, and transitions to T.CLOSE (#715).

## 2. Summary of T5.01 through T5.04

**T5.01 (Network and Topology).** These two terms name emergent structural properties, not designed infrastructure. *Network* is the pattern of Full Aspect Integration (FAI) relationships that emerges as Selves adopt the shared-substrate coordination architecture — it is not a centrally administered overlay. *Topology* is the aggregate of individual participation configurations at any given moment — it is not a centrally administered registry. Both terms carry Claim 6 calibrated-humility status: they describe what the architecture produces at population scale if widely adopted, not what any governance layer directly controls.

**T5.02 (Population, Collective Evolution, Convergence, and Diversity).** All four terms name emergent aggregate properties rather than governed mechanisms. *Population* is the aggregate of FAI-adopting Selves — not an enrolled group managed by a coordinating authority. *Collective evolution* is an emergent consequence of individual substrate evolution across many Selves — not a mechanism any layer directly drives. *Convergence* is a tendency toward pattern similarity that arises from shared architectural constraints, governed at the individual-Self level, not at a population coordination layer. *Diversity* is preserved distinctiveness, protected by the sovereignty of each Self's home governance perimeter — it is an architectural property of the per-Self governance commitment, not a population-level managed outcome. All four carry Claim 6 calibrated-humility status.

**T5.03 (Propagation).** Three distinct senses require separation. Vertical upward propagation — substrate-content changes moving from lower governance levels to higher ones within a Self — is governed under Paper 2 and Paper 3's perimeter structure. Cross-perimeter propagation — a pattern moving from one Self to another via directed selection within a FAI event — is governed under Paper 3's perimeter rules. Network-level pattern spread — a governance approach or substrate pattern spreading across many Selves over time through independent adoption choices — is not governed; it is an emergent aggregate dynamic under Claim 6 calibrated-humility. The governed vs. projected distinction is the critical disambiguation: the first two senses name architectural commitments; the third names a projection.

**T5.04 (Trust Calibration and Governance Capacity Ceiling).** Both terms are governance concepts, not technical ones. *Trust calibration* is a governance quality assessment — the process by which a Self evaluates the governance reliability of a counterpart Self before entering or continuing a FAI relationship. *Governance capacity ceiling* is the upper limit on the number of active inter-Self governance relationships a Self's human governance layer can maintain with adequate oversight, determined by human cognitive and organizational capacity rather than computational load. Both concepts are genuinely new to Paper 3: no equivalent appears in Papers 1 or 2, because the multi-relationship governance management problem they address does not arise at intra-cell or intra-Self scope. Both scale with network size. Both are governed properties — architectural commitments of the inter-Self coordination pattern.

## 3. The two-pattern finding

T5.01 through T5.04 collectively yield two patterns, distinguishable by origin and by registration status in the prior-art corpus.

**Pattern 1 — Genuinely new governance concepts (T5.04).** Trust calibration and governance capacity ceiling are Paper 3-specific architectural commitments with no equivalent in the prior papers. They address a class of governance problem — managing governance quality across multiple simultaneous inter-Self relationships — that is structurally invisible at the intra-Self scope Papers 1 and 2 occupy. These concepts are governed properties: the architecture commits to them as design requirements, not as projected consequences. Their genuine novelty to Paper 3 should be acknowledged rather than absorbed into inheritance from prior papers.

**Pattern 2 — Calibrated-humility projections (T5.01, T5.02, T5.03 Sense 3).** Network, topology, population, collective evolution, convergence, diversity, and network-level pattern propagation are all Claim 6 calibrated-humility projections. They describe emergent population-scale dynamics that the architecture anticipates as a consequence of widespread adoption, not mechanisms the architecture governs directly. Population-scale CKS-governed Self deployments do not yet exist; specific dynamics at that scale await empirical observation. The architecture commits to what the inheritance produces; it does not commit to specific outcomes at the population level.

The two patterns are not equally sized, but both are essential. The genuinely new concepts category is small — two terms from one note. The calibrated-humility projections category covers the majority of population-scale vocabulary. The imbalance is correct, not a gap.

## 4. The register distinction finding

The two-pattern finding yields a register distinction that is the T5 subseries' primary contribution to the prior-art corpus.

Population-scale concepts in the trilogy are not uniformly architectural commitments. Some are governed properties — concepts the architecture commits to as design requirements, exercisable as obligations on any conforming implementation. Others are projected emergent consequences — concepts describing what the architecture anticipates will follow from widespread adoption, stated with calibrated humility appropriate to claims whose empirical verification lies ahead.

This distinction is load-bearing for the prior-art position in two directions. Claiming governed status for calibrated-humility projections overstates the prior art, attributing architectural commitments the papers do not make. Claiming calibrated-humility status for governed properties understates the prior art, erasing architectural commitments the papers do make. T5's contribution is the correct sorting.

The register distinction maps cleanly onto the vocabulary the trilogy established for Paper 3's Claim 6: the architectural commitment is rendered confidently; the empirical-verification status of specific population-scale dynamics is rendered with calibrated humility. T5 operationalizes this distinction at the level of individual terms, producing a term-by-term register assignment across the population-scale vocabulary rather than a claim-level generalization.
