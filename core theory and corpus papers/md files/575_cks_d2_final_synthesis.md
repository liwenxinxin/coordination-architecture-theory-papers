## Abstract

This note is the eightieth and final note of Phase D2 (#496–#575) in the CKS derivation series. Phase D2 produced eighty operational prior-art notes that decompose Paper 3's inter-Self coordination architecture into operationally specific, individually defensible derivations. The notes are organized across six coverage areas: shared substrate lifecycle governance; Full Aspect Integration operation; conflict handling and escalation; evolution feed and home integration; governance quality and standards; and population-scope operations. This note provides a structured summary of each coverage area, confirms that Phase D2's operational prior-art foundation for Paper 3 is complete, and introduces Phase D3, which will produce standalone anti-pattern notes giving full prior-art depth to each named FAI governance failure mode. The combined Phase D0, D1, and D2 corpus — notes #460 through #575, totaling 116 notes — constitutes the core operational prior-art foundation for Paper 3.

## 2. What Phase D2 was for

Paper 3 introduces an inter-Self coordination architecture based on a temporary shared substrate and a named exchange primitive, Full Aspect Integration (FAI). The architecture makes six main claims: that the shared substrate inherits Paper 1's six commitments at inter-Self scope; that FAI is the canonical exchange primitive over it; that conflicts at the inter-Self boundary receive three-tier handling; that FAI events feed back into four evolution loci in each participating Self's home substrate; that all configurable dimensions — including configuration of configuration — are substrate content under human authority; and that population-scale collective evolution is the emergent property of many FAI events across many Selves.

Phase D1 formalized the sub-commitments underlying those six claims. Phase D2 decomposed the sub-commitments further into operationally specific derivations: the particular mechanics, boundary conditions, variants, and governance requirements that any implementation must satisfy. The decomposition strategy follows the pattern established by Series B Phase B2 (Paper 2 operational variants): each D1 sub-commitment produces several D2 notes, each covering a distinct operational dimension that could be separately patented, separately designed, or separately implemented by parties seeking to build inter-Self coordination architectures. The result is a coverage mesh fine enough that no operationally significant dimension of Paper 3's architecture lacks named prior art.

Phase D2 organizes its 80 notes across six coverage areas. The sections below summarize each area.

## 3. Coverage Area 1 — Shared substrate lifecycle governance

**Notes:** D2.01–D2.05, D2.17, D2.28, D2.53, D2.54

Coverage Area 1 addresses the construction and dissolution of the shared substrate — the events that bracket every FAI event and define what the shared substrate is as a temporal object.

D2.01–D2.05 established the foundational lifecycle commitments: construction as an intentional governance act (not an automatic side-effect of communication), the shared substrate's perimeter-spanning property and what it implies for governance authority, the inheritance of all six Paper 1 commitments within the shared substrate's scope of operation, dissolution governance and what happens at the FAI hand-off boundary, and the persistence policy as substrate content that is itself human-governed. These five notes correspond directly to the D1 sub-commitments they decompose.

D2.17 addressed the temporal interleaving of shared substrate events with each participating Self's home governance operations — specifically, the architectural requirement that home governance continues uninterrupted during FAI participation and that no FAI event can suspend or supersede a Self's home governance authority.

D2.28 established lifecycle isolation: what the shared substrate keeps isolated from home substrate content during the event, and why isolation is an architectural commitment rather than an implementation choice.

D2.53 and D2.54 addressed lifecycle and evolution isolation together, covering how the end-of-lifecycle dissolution interacts with each participating Self's evolution mechanisms and how isolation properties carry through from construction to dissolution to home ingestion.

## 4. Coverage Area 2 — FAI operation

**Notes:** D2.06–D2.12, D2.15–D2.16, D2.20–D2.22, D2.25–D2.27, D2.38, D2.42, D2.48, D2.49

Coverage Area 2 is the largest, covering the mechanics of Full Aspect Integration as the canonical exchange operation over the shared substrate. This area addresses how aspects are contributed, how they merge, how cardinality governs multi-Self events, and how FAI mechanics interoperate with content tiers, configuration, and parallel event governance.

D2.06–D2.12 established the core FAI primitives: the aspect as exchange unit and what that implies for the granularity of inter-Self exchange; n-ary cardinality as the architectural commitment accommodating any number of participating Selves; the full-merge default and the three merge pattern variants (full merge, selective merge, read-only access); the exchange bounding commitment (DNA-layer and action-layer content exchange; LLM weights and instinct-layer content do not); the three persistence loci (within the shared substrate during the event, within the shared substrate as a durable post-dissolution record, and within each participating Self's home substrate); and FAI as the inter-Self analog of Paper 2's mating primitive.

D2.15–D2.16 addressed merge pattern selection and configuration authoring — how the choice among the three merge patterns is governed and what makes that governance human-authorable substrate content rather than algorithmic selection.

D2.20–D2.22 covered parallel event governance: what the architecture requires when a Self participates in more than one FAI event concurrently, how parallel events are bounded from each other, and how home governance arbitrates across concurrent participations.

D2.25–D2.27 established asymmetric participation mechanics — the cases where participating Selves contribute different aspects, contribute at different depths, or hold different authority roles within the shared substrate — and how the FAI architecture handles asymmetry without reducing it to symmetric defaults.

D2.38 addressed single-aspect FAI cases — the boundary condition in which only one aspect from each Self exchanges — and confirmed that these cases instantiate the full FAI architecture rather than constituting a separate simpler primitive.

D2.42 covered multi-domain FAI events where the aspects contributed span more than one organizational domain, addressing provenance attribution and conflict handling at domain boundaries.

D2.48 and D2.49 addressed content tier boundaries — the precise demarcation between DNA-layer and action-layer content in the exchange context, and how the boundary applies when aspect content spans layers.

## 5. Coverage Area 3 — Conflict handling and escalation

**Notes:** D2.13–D2.15, D2.45, D2.51, D2.65

Coverage Area 3 addresses the three-tier conflict-handling mechanism at inter-Self scope. Paper 3's three tiers — preserve, resolve via orchestration, escalate to humans — inherit from Paper 1's substrate-level preservation and orchestration-rule-governed resolution, and from Paper 2's escalation patterns. Coverage Area 3 formalizes what the inheritance means operationally and how each tier functions at the inter-Self boundary.

D2.13 established the conflict registry structure within the shared substrate: how conflicts surfaced during FAI are recorded, what provenance they carry, and why their preservation as first-class state is an architectural commitment inherited from Paper 1 rather than an optional record-keeping choice.

D2.14 covered escalation routing and response — the conditions under which the third tier (escalate to humans) triggers, who holds the relevant joint authority, and how the escalation path is itself governance-configured substrate content.

D2.15, which also appears in Coverage Area 2, addressed the interaction between merge pattern selection and conflict surface probability — specifically, how the choice of merge pattern (and the configuration governing it) affects which conflicts the shared substrate will surface and at which tier they are handled.

D2.45 addressed orchestration rule authoring for inter-Self conflict handling — the requirement that the rules governing second-tier resolution be human-authored, inspectable, and stored as substrate content within the shared substrate, not embedded in automated middleware outside governance authority.

D2.51 covered dispute resolution when participating Selves hold incompatible positions on conflict handling — the case where the conflict is not about content but about how a content conflict should be handled — and the architectural requirement that such meta-conflicts escalate to joint human authority rather than being resolved by either Self's home orchestration rules unilaterally.

D2.65 addressed competitive intelligence context: the special governance requirements when FAI events occur between Selves operating in competitive or partially adversarial organizational contexts, and how the conflict registry and escalation tier interact with those contexts.

## 6. Coverage Area 4 — Evolution feed and home integration

**Notes:** D2.10–D2.11, D2.23–D2.26, D2.29, D2.39, D2.52–D2.59

Coverage Area 4 addresses the four-locus evolution feed — the mechanism by which FAI events produce evolution inputs that each participating Self absorbs into its home substrate through Paper 2's evolution mechanisms. This is one of Paper 3's most architecturally significant contributions: FAI events are not isolated coordination episodes; they are inputs to each Self's ongoing evolutionary development.

D2.10–D2.11, which also appear in Coverage Area 2, addressed the action-feedback and DNA-layer absorption paths specifically — how FAI outputs map to Paper 2's action-feedback evolution mechanism (the most direct path) and how DNA-layer content exchanged during FAI maps to Paper 2's DNA evolution mechanism.

D2.23–D2.26 established provenance internalization — how provenance attached to aspects during FAI persists as each participating Self ingests evolution outputs, so that a Self's home substrate retains attribution to the inter-Self event that produced a given content element. D2.23 covered the ingestion commitment; D2.24 addressed provenance depth configuration (how much cross-perimeter provenance a Self retains is governance-configured); D2.25 covered chaining (how provenance from FAI-derived content connects to subsequent evolution events in the home substrate); D2.26 addressed trust calibration (how a receiving Self's home governance weights FAI-derived content relative to home-originated content).

D2.29 addressed post-mortem governance — the requirement that each participating Self conduct a structured reflection on the FAI event's evolution outputs before absorbing them, and how that reflection is itself a governed operation producing substrate content.

D2.39 covered the interaction between the evolution feed and configuration content — specifically, how configuration changes that emerge from FAI events are absorbed into a Self's home substrate through the configuration-as-substrate-content commitment.

D2.52–D2.59 formed a focused cluster on multi-tier evolution and lifecycle interactions: how the four evolution loci interact with each other and with the shared substrate's lifecycle; how evolution outputs from one FAI event can seed future FAI participation; multi-tier evolution mechanics when a Self participates in FAI events at different scales; and how the evolution feed interacts with Paper 2's lineage and supersession commitments.

## 7. Coverage Area 5 — Governance quality and standards

**Notes:** D2.18–D2.19, D2.24, D2.33–D2.37, D2.40, D2.43, D2.66–D2.68, D2.70–D2.77

Coverage Area 5 is the meta-governance area: it addresses not specific FAI operations but the standards a well-governed inter-Self coordination architecture must meet across all operations. This area is the largest by note count after Coverage Area 2, reflecting the breadth of governance quality requirements Paper 3 inherits and extends from the prior papers.

D2.18–D2.19 established the complete governance record requirement — that every FAI event produce a full, inspectable governance record — and named the twelve anti-patterns that represent governance quality failures in inter-Self coordination. The anti-patterns named in D2.19 are the source material that Phase D3 will develop into standalone notes.

D2.24, which also appears in Coverage Area 4, addressed provenance depth configuration as a governance quality commitment.

D2.33–D2.37 established the test suite and portfolio management requirements: how governance quality is verified operationally (D2.33), what constitutes a complete test suite for FAI governance (D2.34), how cross-organizational agreements are formalized as substrate content (D2.35), what health indicators signal governance quality degradation (D2.36), and documentation standards for shared substrate operations (D2.37).

D2.40 established minimum viable governance — the floor below which a shared substrate construction does not satisfy Paper 3's architectural commitments. Minimum viable governance specifies the least a governance implementation must do, not the most; it is a completeness floor, not a design prescription.

D2.43 addressed governance quality for the evolution feed specifically — the standards a well-governed ingestion process must meet to ensure that evolution outputs from FAI events are absorbed without governance regression in the receiving Self's home substrate.

D2.66–D2.68 and D2.70–D2.77 formed a cluster covering the specific governance commitments that Paper 3 inherits from and extends beyond Papers 1 and 2: determinism (D2.66, D2.70), path retraceability (D2.67, D2.71), non-delegation of authority (D2.68, D2.72), three-adjacencies at inter-Self scope (D2.73), the non-specialist principle for inter-Self governance — that governance authority does not require technical specialization (D2.74), the substrate-as-source-of-truth commitment at inter-Self scope (D2.75), and prior-art consolidation notes connecting Phase D2 to the earlier Series A and B derivations that established the same commitments at cell and Self scope (D2.76–D2.77).

## 8. Coverage Area 6 — Population-scope operations

**Notes:** D2.31–D2.32, D2.60–D2.62, D2.64–D2.65, D2.68, D2.71–D2.73

Coverage Area 6 addresses Paper 3's extension claim — Claim 6, population-scale collective evolution — and the operational requirements for governing FAI at the scale of many Selves across a network. Where Coverage Areas 1–5 address any FAI event between any two or more Selves, Coverage Area 6 addresses what becomes architecturally significant when such events are numerous, recurring, and distributed.

D2.31–D2.32 established participation governance at population scale: how a Self's pattern of FAI participation is itself a governance-configured dimension (not emergent behavior), and how joint authority operates across population-level governance perimeters.

D2.60–D2.62 addressed pattern propagation — the mechanism by which governance patterns developed within one FAI event propagate to future events — and parallel FAI governance at scale (how many concurrent FAI events across a population maintain governance isolation from each other without requiring central coordination infrastructure).

D2.64–D2.65 addressed network topology and pattern control modes. D2.64 covered how the topology of FAI relationships across a population is itself governance-configured substrate content. D2.65 covered pattern control — the distinction between emergent coordination patterns (which the architecture does not prohibit but cannot rely upon) and governed coordination patterns (which the architecture requires for compliance with Paper 3's commitments).

D2.68, which also appears in Coverage Area 5, addressed non-delegation specifically in the population-scope context: the requirement that population-level governance authority not be delegated to any automated pattern-propagation mechanism, regardless of scale.

D2.71–D2.73 addressed observational study governance (D2.71), frequency-variant governance requirements when FAI event frequency varies significantly across a population (D2.72), and domain-specific population contexts such as professional and regulated-industry settings where population-scope FAI carries additional governance requirements (D2.73).

## 10. Transition to Phase D3: standalone anti-pattern notes

Phase D3 is the next phase of Series D. It begins with D3.01 (sequence number #576) and is expected to produce approximately 30 notes (#576–#605). Phase D3's function is to give each named FAI governance failure mode its own standalone note, with full prior-art depth: detection criteria, consequence analysis, and prior-art argumentation establishing that the failure mode is a recognized and named anti-pattern in the public record.

The source material for Phase D3 is note D2.19, which named twelve anti-patterns organized under Paper 3's six claims. D2.19 is the Phase D3 taxonomy note: it names each anti-pattern and assigns it to a claim, but does not give each anti-pattern the standalone treatment that a well-defended prior-art note requires. Phase D3 supplies that treatment.

The expected structure of each Phase D3 note is: definition of the anti-pattern (what an implementation must do to instantiate it); detection criteria (what an observer inspecting a system would see); consequence analysis (what governance failures the anti-pattern causes and how they propagate); prior-art argumentation (how Phase D1 and D2 notes, combined with Papers 1–3, establish that the anti-pattern is the recognized failure mode and not merely one possible design choice); and the claim violation it constitutes.

The twelve anti-patterns from D2.19, organized by claim, are the expected Phase D3 note subjects:

For Claim 1 (shared substrate inherits Paper 1 commitments): opaque inter-Self communication — conducting inter-Self coordination through message-passing or shared-memory access outside the shared substrate governance perimeter.

For Claim 2 (FAI as canonical exchange primitive): shallow merge (federation-only participation without genuine aspect exchange); exchange without bounding (allowing instinct-layer or LLM-weight content to enter the exchange); and cardinality collapse (reducing n-ary FAI to fixed bilateral defaults).

For Claim 3 (three-tier conflict handling): silent conflict resolution at the inter-Self boundary — suppressing or automatically resolving conflicts before they reach the preserve or escalate tiers; and single-tier collapse, in which orchestration-resolved and escalation-required conflicts are conflated.

For Claim 4 (four-locus evolution feed): ungoverned evolution feed — absorbing FAI evolution outputs without provenance tracking or home governance review; and provenance erasure — absorbing FAI-derived content into the home substrate with provenance stripped.

For Claim 5 (configuration as substrate content): fixed configuration — maintaining inter-Self configuration parameters outside substrate governance; and meta-governance escape — configuring the configuration-of-configuration outside human authority.

For Claim 6 (population-scale collective evolution): emergent coordination replaces substrate — allowing the population-level coordination pattern to operate through informal channels outside the shared substrate architecture; and pattern propagation without governance — allowing coordination patterns to propagate across Selves without governance configuration at the receiving end.

Phase D3 opens with D3.01 — an overview and anti-pattern taxonomy introduction note — followed by one standalone note per anti-pattern. Some anti-patterns may be developed across two notes where the detection criteria and consequence analysis warrant additional depth.
