## Abstract

Notes CC.01 through CC.06 each formalized one inheritance edge from Paper 3 back to a specific Paper 1 commitment, covering all six of Paper 1's architectural commitments in sequence. This note — CC.07 — synthesizes what those six notes established as a body. It states the universal inheritance pattern that runs across all six notes, characterizes the nature of Paper 3's extension, and consolidates the full P3↔P1 inheritance chain into a single defensible claim: Paper 3 is Paper 1 at inter-Self scope. Every Paper 3 commitment traces to a Paper 1 commitment. Paper 3 does not introduce new architectural principles; it introduces the mechanisms required to apply Paper 1's principles at inter-organizational scope. This consolidated claim is the prior-art summary for adversarial proceedings targeting novelty in inter-Self AI governance coordination.

## 2. What CC.01–CC.06 established

The six notes covered Paper 3's extensions of each of Paper 1's six architectural commitments to inter-Self scope:

**CC.01 — Hybrid commitment.** Paper 1 commits to a hybrid architecture in which the coordination substrate handles governance and coordination while the LLM handles high-dimensional reasoning, with the division drawn at the governance boundary. CC.01 established that Paper 3 extends this commitment to inter-Self scope through exchange bounding — the instinct layer cannot cross organizational boundaries — and through the shared substrate as the outside-the-model coordination spine for inter-Self events. The hybrid division travels unchanged; the scope is larger.

**CC.02 — Conflict preservation.** Paper 1 commits to conflict preservation as first-class substrate state, with two-level handling: substrate-level preservation as default and cell-level resolution under orchestration rules authored by humans. CC.02 established that Paper 3 extends this through a three-tier mechanism — preserve, resolve via orchestration, escalate to joint human authority — and through a carry-through mechanism that routes preserved conflicts as evolution-feed annotations to participating organizations' home substrates. The principle of conflict as first-class addressable state travels unchanged; the mechanism adapts to the cross-perimeter governance structure.

**CC.03 — Human-governed authority.** Paper 1 commits to human authority — the rights to inspect, modify, and override substrate content and orchestration rules at any time — as distinct from human labor. CC.03 established that Paper 3 extends this through joint authority: at inter-Self scope, multiple governance authorities hold the three rights over the shared substrate and its orchestration rules during a coordination event. The authority-versus-labor distinction and the three rights travel unchanged; the authority structure expands from single to joint.

**CC.04 — AI as substrate mediator.** Paper 1 commits to the LLM operating as mediator over substrate content under human-authored orchestration rules, not as an autonomous governance agent. CC.04 established that Paper 3 preserves this role within the shared substrate: LLMs read, write, and compose substrate content under jointly-authored orchestration rules during inter-Self coordination events. The governed mediator role travels unchanged; the governance rules are now jointly authored across participating organizations.

**CC.05 — Tool agnosticism.** Paper 1 commits to platform independence at the substrate layer: any environment that supports persistent structured state, human read/write access, and LLM access to substrate content can instantiate the architecture. CC.05 established that Paper 3 extends this to the shared substrate used during inter-Self coordination events, with platform independence holding at both single-Self and shared-substrate scope, and domain agnosticism holding across the full range of organizational contexts in which inter-Self events may occur. The three minimal requirements travel unchanged; they now govern the shared substrate as well.

**CC.06 — Path retraceability.** Paper 1 commits to a complete provenance chain for every substrate operation. CC.06 established that Paper 3 extends this to a four-link cross-organizational authorization chain: the home-governance authorization in each participating organization, the joint-authority authorization for the shared substrate event, the LLM mediation step within the shared substrate, and the per-home ingestion authorization at dissolution. The completeness requirement for the provenance chain travels unchanged; the chain spans organizational boundaries.

## 3. The universal inheritance pattern

Across all six notes, a single structural pattern repeats without exception.

**What is always preserved** is the architectural principle. The outside-the-model governance spine, conflict as first-class state, structural human authority over substrate content and rules, the LLM as governed mediator, platform independence at the substrate layer, and the completeness of the provenance chain — all six of Paper 1's defining commitments carry through to Paper 3 intact. None is weakened. None is replaced. None is treated as inapplicable at inter-Self scope.

**What is always extended** is the scope and the governance authority structure. In Paper 1, each commitment operates within a single deployment governed by a single authority. In Paper 3, each commitment operates across multiple organizations governed by multiple authorities. The extension from one Self to multiple Selves, and from single authority to joint authority, is what changes. The principle itself does not.

This pattern — principles preserved, scope and authority structure extended — is the universal shape of the P3↔P1 inheritance chain. No exception to it appeared across the six notes. The pattern is not incidental; it reflects a deliberate architectural posture in Paper 3, which explicitly accounts for its inheritance from Paper 1 at each claim and characterizes its own contributions as principles-extending rather than principles-replacing.

## 4. The nature of Paper 3's extension

Paper 3 does not add new architectural principles to the ones Paper 1 established. What Paper 3 adds are the mechanisms required for applying Paper 1's principles at inter-organizational scope. These mechanisms are not the same thing as principles.

The mechanisms Paper 3 adds include: exchange bounding (the commitment that instinct-layer and LLM-weight content does not cross the inter-Self perimeter, as an inter-Self articulation of Paper 2's instinct/reasoning separation); three-tier conflict handling (the cross-perimeter conflict resolution architecture required when conflicts surface across organizational governance boundaries); joint authority (the governance structure by which multiple organizations hold authority over a shared substrate under jointly-authored rules); the four-locus evolution feed (the routing mechanism that returns FAI-event learning to each participating organization's home evolution machinery after the shared substrate dissolves); and configuration as substrate content at inter-Self scope (the recursive-governance commitment that the rules governing inter-Self events are themselves first-class substrate content subject to the three rights).

Each mechanism is operational. Each is required by the larger scope. None of them constitutes a principle Paper 1 did not already commit to. Exchange bounding implements the instinct/reasoning separation at inter-Self scope. Three-tier conflict handling implements conflict-as-first-class-state at inter-Self scope under joint authority. Joint authority implements structural human governance at inter-Self scope. The four-locus evolution feed implements path retraceability and substrate-as-source-of-truth at the event boundary. Configuration as substrate content implements tool-agnosticism and human-governed authority recursively.

The distinction between mechanism and principle is architecturally significant and defensively important. A claim that Paper 3 introduced a novel conflict resolution mechanism, for instance, must address CC.02's documentation of three-tier conflict handling as the direct extension of Paper 1's two-level handling through the addition of joint human authority at the escalation tier. A claim that joint authority is novel must address CC.03's documentation of joint authority as the extension of Paper 1's single-authority governance structure to the multi-organization context. The mechanisms are new at their scope; the principles they implement are not.

## 5. The consolidated P3↔P1 inheritance claim

The six individual inheritance notes together support a single consolidated claim:

**Paper 3's inter-Self coordination architecture is Paper 1's single-Self coordination architecture applied to the inter-organizational governance context.**

Every Paper 3 commitment traces to a Paper 1 commitment. No Paper 3 commitment is architecturally novel relative to Paper 1's principles; each is an extension of a Paper 1 principle to a larger scope with an adapted governance authority structure. The mechanisms Paper 3 adds are required by the larger scope; they are operational implementations of Paper 1's principles, not departures from them.

This claim is the prior-art summary for adversarial proceedings. Any challenge to the novelty of inter-Self AI governance coordination must address all six inheritance edges documented in CC.01 through CC.06. The chain is comprehensive: it covers the hybrid commitment, conflict preservation, human authority, AI-as-mediator, platform independence, and provenance completeness. There is no Paper 1 architectural commitment that Paper 3 bypasses or redefines.

The consolidated claim may be stated compactly: **Paper 3 is Paper 1 at inter-Self scope.** This is the single-sentence formulation of what the six individual inheritance notes establish in detail.
