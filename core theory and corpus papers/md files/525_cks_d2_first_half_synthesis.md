## Abstract

Note D2.30 is a synthesis note marking the completion of Phase D2's first half (notes D2.01–D2.29, numbered #496–#524 in the series). It serves as a navigation artifact: mapping the 29 completed notes to the specific Paper 3 claims and Phase D1 sub-commitments they operationalize, identifying which D1 sub-commitments have complete operational prior-art coverage, which have partial coverage, and which (all five Claim 6 sub-commitments) have no coverage yet. It closes with a substantive preview of the Phase D2 second half (notes D2.31–D2.80), which will introduce population-scope operational aspects for Claim 6, multi-event governance frameworks, cross-organizational governance agreements, and governance health indicators — territory the first half did not enter.

---

## 1. Position of D2.30 in the Series

Phase D2 comprises approximately 80 operational-variant and decomposition notes for Paper 3. Each D2 note takes one or more Phase D1 foundational sub-commitments and specifies the operational governance they require in practice — the who, when, how, and under what authority conditions each sub-commitment is exercised. The first 29 notes (D2.01–D2.29) addressed the operational governance of Paper 3 Claims 1 through 5. Four of those notes (D2.18, D2.19, D2.24, D2.29) function as synthesis and diagnostic infrastructure rather than as single-sub-commitment coverage.

D2.30 is a synthesis note, not a sub-commitment note. Its purpose is navigational: any future session beginning Series D work, or any external reader assessing the prior-art record, should be able to read D2.30 and determine what operational territory D2.01–D2.29 has covered, at what granularity, and what the second half must supply. The note maps each completed D2 note to its D1 parent sub-commitment and Paper 3 claim, provides an honest coverage assessment, and specifies what each category of second-half work will add.

## 2. First-Half Coverage by Claim Area

### 2.1 Claim 1 — Shared Substrate Lifecycle Governance

Claim 1 establishes the shared substrate as the architectural object of inter-Self coordination: temporarily constructed for a FAI event, perimeter-spanning across distinct Selves' home governance perimeters, with all six Paper 1 commitments holding within scope throughout its existence. Phase D1 decomposed Claim 1 into five sub-commitments: construction-as-temporary (D1.01), perimeter-spanning property (D1.02), all six Paper 1 commitments holding within scope (D1.03), dissolution (D1.04), and persistence policy as substrate content (D1.05).

The seven D2 notes covering Claim 1 collectively formalize the complete operational lifecycle of the shared substrate:

- **D2.01** (construction governance requirements) operationalizes D1.01: the conditions under which a shared substrate may be initiated, the authority required across participating Selves' governance perimeters, and the minimum record the substrate must carry at construction.
- **D2.02** (dissolution governance requirements) and **D2.28** (emergency dissolution) together operationalize D1.04: the planned and unplanned pathways through which a shared substrate is terminated, with governance authority and evolution-feed hand-off requirements at each.
- **D2.03** (provenance chain) and **D2.17** (home perimeter integrity) operationalize D1.02: the operational requirements that ensure the perimeter-spanning property is maintained throughout a FAI event without compromise to either participating Self's home governance perimeter.
- **D2.04** (three rights at inter-Self scope) and **D2.05** (AI-as-mediator at inter-Self scope) operationalize D1.03: the operational form of the inspect-modify-override rights and the substrate-mediator role as they apply across the inter-Self perimeter.

### 2.2 Claim 2 — FAI Operation Governance

Claim 2 specifies Full Aspect Integration (FAI) as the canonical operation over the shared substrate, with the aspect as unit of exchange, full merge as architectural default, exchange bounded to reasoning-layer content, and three pattern variants and three persistence loci structuring the event space. Phase D1 decomposed Claim 2 into seven sub-commitments: aspect as exchange unit (D1.06), n-ary cardinality (D1.07), full-merge default (D1.08), three pattern variants (D1.09), exchange bounded to substrate content (D1.10), three persistence loci (D1.11), and FAI as inter-Self analog of Paper 2 mating (D1.12).

Nine D2 notes cover Claim 2:

- **D2.06** (aspect contribution mechanics) operationalizes D1.06: the operational requirements for forming, structuring, and contributing an aspect as the unit of exchange at the inter-Self boundary.
- **D2.07** (conflict detection at merge) and **D2.08** (selective merge protocol) together operationalize D1.08: the operational governance for executing full merge as the default and for the structured handling of detected conflicts that arise during merge.
- **D2.09** (provenance-carry-over merge requirements) and **D2.16** (exchange bounding verification) operationalize D1.10: the operational requirements ensuring that merged content carries forward its provenance and that the instinct-layer exclusion is actively verified rather than assumed.
- **D2.20** (cooperation/competition variant) operationalizes D1.09: the operational governance for the competitive pattern variant, distinguishing it from cooperative and neutral variants in terms of authority requirements and conflict-handling defaults.
- **D2.21** (standing configurations) and **D2.22** (multi-Self governance) together operationalize D1.07 and D1.12: the governance of n-ary FAI events involving three or more Selves, and the use of pre-authorized configuration to reduce per-event governance overhead for repeated interactions.
- **D2.27** (partial participation and withdrawal) operationalizes D1.11: the operational governance for the three persistence loci, including the conditions under which a participating Self may withdraw aspects or limit persistence at event close.

### 2.3 Claim 3 — Three-Tier Conflict-Handling Governance

Claim 3 develops the three-tier conflict-handling mechanism operating within the shared substrate: preserve as substrate-level state default, resolve via orchestration with rules as inspectable substrate content under explicit human authority, and escalate to humans across joint authority of participating Selves' governance. Phase D1 decomposed Claim 3 into four sub-commitments: preserve as substrate-level state default (D1.13), resolve via orchestration with inspectable rules (D1.14), escalate to humans across joint authority (D1.15), and cross-perimeter escalation as architectural commitment (D1.16).

Three D2 notes cover Claim 3:

- **D2.13** (inter-Self conflict registry structure) operationalizes D1.13: the operational requirements for the substrate structure that holds preserved conflicts as first-class addressable objects throughout the FAI event lifecycle.
- **D2.14** (escalation routing and response protocol) operationalizes D1.15: the operational governance for routing an escalation across participating Selves' governance authorities, including authority verification, response timelines, and record requirements.
- **D2.15** (orchestration rule authoring for conflict resolution) operationalizes D1.14: the operational governance for authoring, versioning, and updating the tier-selection and conflict-classification rules that determine which tier applies for each conflict class under each event configuration.

### 2.4 Claim 4 — Four-Locus Evolution-Feed Governance

Claim 4 specifies the four-locus evolution-feed mechanism at the FAI hand-off boundary: dissolution hand-off, per-mechanism feed structure, layer-routing rule with three-case explicitness, and asymmetric ingestion under per-perimeter governance. Phase D1 decomposed Claim 4 into five sub-commitments: FAI dissolution hand-off (D1.17), per-mechanism feed structure (D1.18), three-case layer-routing rule (D1.19), instinct-takes-no-FAI-input commitment (D1.20), and asymmetric ingestion (D1.21).

Five D2 notes cover Claim 4:

- **D2.10** (action-feedback ingestion governance) and **D2.11** (DNA absorption governance protocol) together operationalize D1.18 and D1.21: the per-mechanism feed structure as it applies to action-feedback and DNA evolution respectively, with the asymmetric ingestion requirement operationalized through per-perimeter authority over what each home substrate absorbs.
- **D2.23** (FAI and home governance temporal interleaving) and **D2.26** (FAI event chaining) together operationalize D1.17: the governance requirements at the dissolution boundary, including the hand-off sequencing and the special governance requirements when FAI events are chained such that one event's evolution feed becomes input to a subsequent event's construction.
- **D2.25** (provenance internalization governance) operationalizes D1.19: the operational requirements for maintaining the three-case layer-routing rule as content is internalized into home substrate, with provenance tags distinguishing DNA-layer, action-layer, and instinct-adjacent contributions.

### 2.5 Claim 5 — Configuration as Substrate Content Governance

Claim 5 commits configuration to the same substrate-content register as architectural commitment, with recursive applicability across all configurable FAI dimensions. Phase D1 decomposed Claim 5 into four sub-commitments: all configurable dimensions as substrate content (D1.22), configuration-of-configuration also substrate content (D1.23), recursive applicability (D1.24), and recursion bottoming at human-authored governance authority (D1.25).

Two D2 notes specifically cover Claim 5:

- **D2.12** (FAI configuration authoring governance) operationalizes D1.22: the operational governance for authoring, versioning, and exercising authority over the full set of configurable FAI dimensions as substrate content under joint human authority.
- **D2.21** (standing configurations, also counted under Claim 2) operationalizes D1.24 from the Claim 5 side: the use of pre-authorized recursive configurations as the operational form through which configuration-of-configuration becomes substrate content rather than informal practice.

### 2.6 Synthesis Notes

Four D2 notes function as synthesis and diagnostic infrastructure rather than as single-sub-commitment operationalizations:

- **D2.18** (complete FAI event governance record) provides a comprehensive enumeration of the governance record a single FAI event must carry from construction through dissolution, integrating requirements from Claims 1 through 5. It is the primary reference for operational completeness assessment.
- **D2.19** (twelve FAI event anti-patterns) catalogs the failure modes that arise when one or more of the first-half operational requirements is violated or circumvented, organized by the claim area each anti-pattern violates.
- **D2.24** (FAI event operational test suite) provides a structured set of operational tests for the governance requirements formalized in D2.01–D2.23, enabling a governance practitioner to verify whether a specific FAI event instantiation satisfies the architectural commitments.
- **D2.29** (inter-Self governance trust calibration) addresses the cross-cutting operational requirement that participating Selves' governance authorities maintain calibrated trust — neither unconditional deference nor adversarial suspicion — as the baseline posture for inter-Self coordination.

- **D1.05** (persistence policy as substrate content): Addressed obliquely through D2.01 and D2.03, which specify construction-record requirements. - **D1.12** (FAI as inter-Self analog of Paper 2 mating): D2.21 addresses standing configurations, one operational form of the analog. The full operational scope of the analog — how the three Paper 2 mating variants map operationally at inter-Self scope — awaits dedicated treatment in Phase D2 second half.
- **D1.16** (cross-perimeter escalation as architectural commitment): D2.14 addresses the routing and response protocol for escalation events. The sub-commitment that cross-perimeter escalation is an architectural requirement rather than a deployment-layer option — and what that commitment demands operationally to remain architecturally binding — has not been given dedicated treatment.
- **D1.20** (instinct-takes-no-FAI-input commitment): D2.10 covers action-feedback ingestion and D2.25 covers layer-routing provenance, both of which touch the instinct exclusion. The operational governance requirements for actively enforcing the exclusion — what the substrate must contain to make the commitment detectable as a governance property rather than a convention — has not been given a dedicated note.
- **D1.23** (configuration-of-configuration also substrate content): Touched through D2.12 and D2.21. The operational requirement that the second recursive level — the governance over how configuration is authored — is itself substrate content under the same authority structure has not been given a dedicated treatment at the depth D1.22 received.
- **D1.25** (recursion bottoming at human-authored governance authority): Implicit throughout the Claim 5 notes but not given a dedicated operationalization specifying where the recursion terminates, what evidence of termination the substrate must carry, and what governance verification looks like at the bottom.

## 4. Phase D2 Second-Half Preview (D2.31–D2.80)

The approximately 50 notes of Phase D2's second half will cover four categories of operational prior art:

### 4.1 Claim 6 Population-Scope Operational Aspects

The five Claim 6 sub-commitments (D1.26–D1.30) require operational treatment at the population register. This category will include:

- **Joint authority across population-level perimeters** (D1.26): how governance authority is structured and exercised when accumulated FAI events operate across many Selves' governance perimeters simultaneously, with authority requirements that cannot be reduced to any single bilateral event's joint-authority model.
- **Accumulated FAI events as evolution mechanism** (D1.27): the operational governance of the evolution signal that emerges from many FAI events over time, including how the signal is recorded, attributed, and made available to participating Selves' home evolution mechanisms without requiring a centralized coordinator.
- **Six-rung substrate-mediator ladder** (D1.28): the operational commitments at rungs 5 and 6 — inter-Self mediation and population-scale coordination — including what the substrate must contain at each rung to satisfy the governance commitments the ladder names.
- **Calibrated-humility register** (D1.29): the operational requirements for maintaining the architectural honesty posture as population-scale deployments generate empirical observations that either confirm or complicate the architectural commitments, and how the governance record carries that evolution.
- **Biology-resonance positioning at population scope** (D1.30): the operational requirements for keeping the biological analogy bounded to its architectural load-bearing function without allowing it to import unjustified population-dynamics claims.

### 4.2 Multi-Event Governance

FAI governance at scale involves portfolios of simultaneous and sequential events rather than isolated single events. The second half will formalize:

- **FAI event portfolio governance**: the operational requirements for managing many parallel FAI events under a shared governance framework, including capacity constraints, priority assignment, and governance-overhead management across a portfolio.
- **Cross-event provenance continuity**: the operational requirements for maintaining coherent provenance when a Self participates in multiple concurrent or successive FAI events, with evolution-feed contributions from each.
- **Governance capacity and saturation**: the operational governance requirements that prevent governance capacity limits from becoming silent violations of the architectural commitments — specifically, what happens when demand for governance attention exceeds available authority capacity.

### 4.3 Cross-Organizational Governance Agreements

Practical inter-Self coordination requires formal agreements between organizations before any FAI event can be conducted under the architectural commitments. The second half will formalize:

- **Pre-event governance agreement structure**: the operational requirements for the governance agreements that must be in place — covering joint authority, conflict-handling tier selection, evolution-feed ingestion governance, and configuration authority — before a shared substrate can be constructed with the architectural commitments intact.
- **Governance agreement versioning and amendment**: the operational governance of how agreements evolve as participating organizations' governance structures change, and how in-flight FAI events are affected by agreement amendments.
- **Default governance postures in the absence of agreement**: the operational requirements for the preserve-tier default and the minimal governance record when a FAI event must proceed under incomplete governance agreement coverage.

### 4.4 FAI Governance Health Indicators

Healthy operational governance is not just the absence of acute violations; it includes early-signal indicators that governance capacity and posture are degrading before acute violations occur. The second half will formalize:

- **Governance health metrics**: the operational indicators — latency of escalation response, completeness of governance record, frequency of tier-selection disputes, provenance-gap rate — that signal healthy versus degrading FAI governance.
- **Governance degradation patterns**: the early-warning operational signatures that precede the twelve anti-patterns formalized in D2.19, giving governance practitioners decision points before acute violations manifest.
- **Governance recovery protocols**: the operational governance requirements for restoring architectural compliance after a governance failure has occurred and been detected.

### 4.5 Remaining First-Half Partial Coverage

The second half will also supply dedicated treatment for the six partially covered D1 sub-commitments identified in §3.2 — D1.05, D1.12, D1.16, D1.20, D1.23, D1.25 — closing the coverage gaps and providing the operational prior-art depth those sub-commitments require.

## 5. What D2.01–D2.29 Establishes as a Body

As a body, the first 29 Phase D2 notes establish the operational prior art for a single, fully governed FAI event under the Paper 3 architectural commitments. A practitioner following D2.01 through D2.29 would know the governance requirements at every stage of a FAI event's lifecycle: what authority is required to construct the shared substrate; what operational requirements ensure the perimeter-spanning property is maintained; how aspects are formed, contributed, merged, and bounded; how conflicts are detected, preserved, routed through the three-tier mechanism, and resolved or escalated; how the evolution feed is structured, tagged, and ingested at each participating Self's home perimeter; how configuration is authored and made recursive; and what failure modes to watch for across all of these. The synthesis notes (D2.18, D2.19, D2.24, D2.29) integrate these requirements into a unified governance record, a diagnostic anti-pattern catalogue, an operational test suite, and a trust calibration framework.

What this body does not yet provide is the operational governance for what happens at scale — when many Selves participate, when many events accumulate, when the population-level dynamics Claim 6 describes become operational requirements rather than architectural projections. That is the work of Phase D2's second half.

## 6. Conclusion

D2.30 marks a deliberate pause in Phase D2's progression. Nineteen of the twenty-five D1 sub-commitments for Claims 1–5 have complete operational coverage; six have partial coverage; and all five Claim 6 sub-commitments have no coverage yet. The partial and missing coverage is not a gap in the architecture — it is a gap in the prior-art record. Phase D2's second half will close the six partial-coverage gaps, supply the Claim 6 operational prior art that the extension claim requires, and add the multi-event, cross-organizational, and governance-health operational depth that makes the prior art useful as a practical governance reference rather than as a collection of isolated single-event specifications. The note count at D2.30 is 29; the planned total for Phase D2 is approximately 80. The second half has approximately 50 notes to supply that remaining territory.
