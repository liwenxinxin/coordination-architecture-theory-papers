## Abstract

Paper 1 (Li, 2026a) defended the Coordination Knowledge Substrate (CKS) as a design pattern at the coordination and decision layer, with the substrate handling coordination and governance, the LLM handling high-dimensional reasoning, and the division drawn at the governance boundary at cell scope. Paper 2 (Li, 2026b) extended CKS to AI Self scope and toward the enterprise brain Self with the foundational separation of fast-pattern instinct from deliberate reasoning into independently-evolving layers under unified human governance. This paper extends substrate-mediated coordination one further step — across the inter-Self perimeter and at population scope — defending six architectural commitments.

The first establishes the **shared substrate** as the architectural object through which two or more CKS-governed Selves coordinate — temporarily constructed for an interaction, perimeter-spanning across distinct Selves' home governance perimeters, with all six Paper 1 commitments holding within scope. The second commits **Full Aspect Integration (FAI)** as the canonical operation over the shared substrate — n-ary aspect-level merge with full-merge as architectural default, exchange bounded to reasoning-layer content per Paper 2's instinct/reasoning separation extended at the inter-Self boundary. The third commits **three-tier conflict handling** within the shared substrate — preserve as substrate-level state by default; resolve via orchestration with rules as inspectable substrate content under explicit human authority; escalate to humans across joint authority of participating Selves' governance. The fourth commits a **four-locus evolution-feed mechanism** connecting inter-Self events to home-perimeter Paper 2 evolution machinery — hand-off at FAI dissolution, per-mechanism feed structure, a three-case layer-routing rule sending DNA-layer content to DNA evolution and action-layer content to action-feedback evolution while instinct evolution takes no FAI input by architectural commitment, and asymmetric ingestion sourced from per-perimeter governance. The fifth commits **configuration as substrate content with recursive applicability** — every dimension of the FAI mechanism is itself substrate content under all six Paper 1 commitments, with the recursion bottoming at human-authored governance authority. The sixth offers the extension claim that **population-scale collective evolution** is the architectural object Paper 3's five preceding claims compose into at population register when accumulated FAI events across many CKS-governed Selves operate under joint authority across population-level governance perimeters. Population-scale CKS-governed Self deployments do not yet exist; the architectural commitment is to what the inheritance produces at population scope through composition, with specific population-scale dynamics awaiting empirical observation — the architectural commitment rendered confidently, the empirical-verification status of the population-scale extension rendered with calibrated humility.

The contribution is a design pattern, not an empirical evaluation. The unifying architectural move across all six claims is the extension of substrate-mediated coordination across the inter-Self perimeter under unified human governance, composed at population scope through accumulated FAI events under joint authority across population-level governance perimeters — Paper 1's *outside-the-model vs. inside-the-model* spine extended through the inter-Self perimeter and into population-scale collective evolution. Paper 3 inherits Papers 1 and 2's architectural commitments and extends them one further step at trilogy completion, sitting at the intersection of multi-agent systems and inter-agent coordination, federated learning and cross-organizational ML, governance-as-code and policy-as-substrate work, multi-coordinator and joint-authority architectures, substrate-as-content and recursive-governance literature, and trilogy continuity with Papers 1 and 2 — articulating a design pattern available to be adopted, varied, composed with adjacent patterns, or argued against. Empirical validation through real-world cross-organizational deployment at scale is downstream work.

# 1. Introduction

## 1.1 Problem framing — the coordination gap at the inter-Self perimeter

Paper 1 (Li, 2026a) defended the Coordination Knowledge Substrate (CKS) as a design pattern at the coordination and decision layer of an AI system, with substrate handling coordination and governance, the LLM handling high-dimensional reasoning, and the division drawn at the governance boundary rather than at the capability boundary. Paper 1 stood at cell scope — a unit of coordinated work, demonstrated through one proof-of-concept in regulated-industry reporting in commodity tools. Paper 2 (Li, 2026b) took Paper 1's modular cell as starting unit and extended the pattern to the AI Self — cells composing into aspects, aspects into Selves, all under unified human governance, with the foundational separation of fast-pattern instinct from deliberate reasoning into independently-evolving layers. Paper 2 closed at the enterprise brain Self as architecturally coherent design pattern, rendered concretely through one thought experiment continuing Paper 1's POC lineage at enterprise scope, with empirical validation in real-world enterprise deployment as downstream work.

What Papers 1 and 2 did not address is what happens when the coordinated whole stops being the largest architectural object the design pattern reasons about. Paper 2 explicitly bracketed inter-Self autonomous dynamics as out of scope — how multiple enterprise brain Selves interact across organizational boundaries was left as a class of extension theories that would each stand on its own claim set rather than on arguments Paper 2 develops. Two further scope-shift directions sit outside Papers 1 and 2 jointly and motivate Paper 3. First, Selves owned by distinct organizations under distinct authority structures encounter each other and have coordination work to do that crosses the boundary between them — work that involves substantive content, conflicts, decisions, and rationale that no single Self holds authority over. Second, many such inter-Self interactions accumulate across a network of CKS-governed Selves over time, producing population-scale dynamics whose architectural-pattern shape — what propagates, what diverges, what governance configurations enable or constrain — is itself a question.

The architectural question is real and is sharpening rather than fading as multi-agent AI deployment matures. Contemporary architectures address inter-AI coordination at scale, but commit to it at registers that leave the substrate-content commitment Paper 1 establishes outside the architectural pattern. Foundation lab multi-agent systems at scale (OpenAI AgentKit, Anthropic Claude multi-agent with MCP, Google Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform) deliver functional coordination through capability scaling and platform-plus-protocol selection, with governance pushed out to deployment-shape and contract/institutional registers. Agent-protocol ecosystems (MCP, A2A, ANP, IBM ACP, IETF and W3C drafts at protocol-spec register) standardize communication mechanics with governance left to implementers. Federated-learning consortia at hundreds-of-organizations scale (FLARE, FATE, OpenFL across healthcare, pharma, government labs, financial sector) deliver cross-organizational coordination through coordinator-mediated aggregation under consortium-governance contracts. Cross-organizational governance frameworks (Gaia-X federation services, IDS-RAM, data trusts and cooperatives, JAMS AI Disputes Clauses and Rules — JAMS being a US-based arbitration provider supplying AI-specific dispute-resolution mechanisms) commit joint authority at contract, institutional, or protocol-parameter layer rather than as substrate-content architectural commitment. Each of these supplies functional inter-AI coordination at progressively larger scopes; none commits at architectural-pattern register to the configuration in which the exchange medium is itself first-class human-governed substrate content carrying Paper 1's six commitments within scope.

Paper 3's response is not that these contemporary architectures should be displaced — they deliver functional coordination at scope honestly. The response is that some commitments — substrate-content commitment for the medium of coordination, joint authority across distinct governance perimeters as architectural property, conflict preservation as first-class state, recursive applicability of configuration as substrate content — must remain architecturally present in the inter-AI exchange itself, for the same reasons Paper 1 named at cell scope and Paper 2 extended at Self scope, but now at a third scope: the inter-Self perimeter and, by composition, the population register. The unifying architectural move is the extension of substrate-mediated coordination across the inter-Self perimeter under unified human governance, with a temporary shared substrate as the medium of coordination, Full Aspect Integration as the canonical operation over it, three-tier conflict handling within, an evolution-feed connecting events to home-perimeter Paper 2 evolution machinery, configuration recursively applicable as substrate content, all composing at population scope as collective evolution under joint authority across population-level governance perimeters. Paper 1's *outside-the-model vs. inside-the-model* spine extends through Paper 3 unchanged: inter-Self coordination, governance, and conflict-handling live in the shared substrate outside any single LLM; instinct-layer content remains within each Self's LLM and does not cross the inter-Self perimeter; and the architecture's value at inter-Self and population scope comes from the same principled separation Papers 1 and 2 defend at their respective scopes.

Paper 3 engages biology lightly and only at one place: Claim 6's framing. The claim that collective interaction across many participants is a primary driver of population-level evolution has biological resonance — species-level evolution in biology is substantially driven by interactions across organisms, not only by within-organism dynamics. Paper 3 borrows this resonance for Claim 6's framing, where inter-Self FAI events accumulating across a network constitute a collective evolution mechanism analogous to (not identical to) collective interaction's role in biological evolution. The core architectural construction Paper 3 develops in §§4–8 has no clean biological parallel. Biology has no governable shared substrate between species; cross-species exchange in biology operates through entirely different mechanisms — horizontal gene transfer, symbiosis, predation, competition for resources — that share neither structure nor governance with the substrate-mediated inter-Self coordination Paper 3 commits to. Paper 3's substrate-mediated inter-Self coordination is one of the architectural features the trilogy specifies as exceeding biology — a degree of freedom CKS provides because the substrate is engineered rather than evolved. The biology framing therefore stays bounded to Claim 6 and does not extend into the core claims.

The mechanism-names-vs-biology-terms convention §7 establishes is carried forward at Paper 3's voice register throughout. ## 3.1 Multi-agent systems and inter-agent coordination

The closest research lineage for Paper 3's inter-Self coordination is contemporary multi-agent systems and inter-agent coordination at scale. Foundation lab multi-agent systems supply the most visible end of this lineage: OpenAI AgentKit, Anthropic Claude multi-agent with MCP, and Google Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform deliver functional coordination across many agents at deployment scope through capability scaling, platform integration, and protocol selection. Kim et al. (2025) derives quantitative scaling principles for multi-agent coordination across more than 180 configurations, with a predictive model selecting optimal coordination architecture for the majority of unseen tasks — explicit articulation of the scaling-science enterprise's stance toward governance as engineering performance kept outside the scaling claim. Agent-protocol ecosystems supply the standardization layer at protocol-spec register: MCP standardizes contextual exchange under cross-vendor stewardship; A2A (Google) supplies standardized peer-to-peer agent-to-agent communication with a fifty-plus enterprise partner ecosystem; ANP, IBM ACP, and IETF and W3C drafts at the protocol-spec register supply complementary specifications across vendor and standards-body boundaries. Agent frameworks supply the framework-API register for composing agent behavior: LangGraph commits to typed shared state with directed graph routing, per-key reducer logic, and durable execution with checkpointing — the closest contemporary multi-agent framework primitive to typed coordination state at substrate-pattern register; AutoGen, CrewAI, Letta, and MemGPT span agent-memory and orchestration territory with varied commitments to message-list aggregation, hierarchical task delegation, and tiered memory. Decentralized cross-organizational frameworks supply a contemporary point of departure from centralized topologies: AgentNet (Yang et al. NeurIPS 2025) commits to decentralized DAG topology with privacy-preserving cross-organizational collaboration. ## 4.2 The named foil — opaque agent-to-agent communication and its structural limits

The foil Claim 1 positions against is the dominant pattern of inter-AI exchange in 2024–2026 multi-agent and inter-agent-protocol systems: communication conducted through messages, untyped state diffs, tool-call payloads, or memory-access patterns whose substantive content is not first-class human-governable substrate. Articulating the foil requires architectural-pattern-register engagement with the contemporary critical literature, not capability or implementation-specific limits. The literature converges on a structural assessment.

The Vector Institute's "Transparency in Agentic AI" survey characterizes most multi-agent frameworks as trajectory-opaque: they expose final outputs and tool traces but do not make multi-agent dialogue and routing decisions first-class queryable objects, leaving what Raza and colleagues describe as a trajectory-level interpretability gap. Institutional AI work on governing collusion in multi-agent LLM markets introduces a separate governance graph and append-only governance log specifically because ungoverned baseline frameworks lack formal representation of origin, authority, or sanctions attached to inter-agent messages. AgentAuditor (2026) introduces a reasoning tree because existing frameworks discard the evidential structure of reasoning traces through majority-vote aggregation. The TechRxiv survey on memory in LLM-based multi-agent systems observes that multi-agent memory infrastructures store information as chunks or messages without persistent queryable links to the originating agent or decision point. Practitioner literature crystallizes the same pattern: Atlan's 2026 "Agent Memory Architectures" comparison observes that "governance is mostly bolted on, not part of the architecture," with the qualification that governed catalogs and knowledge graphs can enforce access control but that the governance comes from the data platform rather than the multi-agent framework itself.

The synthesis across these sources distills four structural gaps: typed governance-aware inter-agent substrate; first-class causal traces and distributed provenance; enforceable permission and compartmentalization models; dedicated oversight and control plane. The properties name the foil structurally — the medium of inter-agent exchange is not first-class human-governable substrate content, and governance is treated as a wrapper around an opaque or semi-opaque exchange. What matters for Claim 1 is that these are architecture-class findings rather than capability-debt findings. If the foil's limits were capability gaps closable by better LLMs or richer protocols, the architectural response would be premature — sharper LLMs and richer protocols would absorb the substrate Claim 1 commits to. The foil literature denies that reading. The limits are properties of the architecture class, where governance is layered around an exchange whose substantive content is not authored substrate. The appropriate response is therefore architectural, and Claim 1 names one: the medium of inter-Self coordination is itself first-class human-governed substrate content with all six Paper 1 commitments holding within scope.

## 4.4 Three differentiation axes

The differentiation work above runs on three architectural axes simultaneously. The axes are independent — each holds against neighbors that meet others — and the conjunction is where the architectural contribution lives. The pattern follows Paper 1 §5.2 (OIDA differentiation) and Paper 2 §4.4 (SOFAI differentiation): three axes carrying differentiation jointly, with the *architecturally different from X on Y axis, not X plus Y* framing rendering the conjunction.

The first axis is **exchange-medium-as-substrate-content versus exchange-medium-as-message, tool, or memory-trace**. In the shared substrate, the medium of inter-Self exchange is itself human-governed substrate content from the start, with all six Paper 1 commitments holding within scope. In multi-agent LLM frameworks, the medium is messages, shared state, or tool-call payloads (AutoGen GroupChat, CrewAI task descriptions, LangGraph state dicts, OpenAI Agents SDK handoffs). In inter-agent protocols, the medium is envelope traffic at protocol-spec register (A2A, MCP). In none is the medium itself first-class human-governable substrate content; governance is applied as a wrapper around an opaque or semi-opaque exchange. This axis is most load-bearing against the named foil and against the multi-agent-AI restatement objection. Claim 1 is architecturally different from LangGraph on the substrate-as-content axis, not LangGraph plus governance.

The second axis is **governance-perimeter-spanning with configuration as substrate content versus opaque cross-perimeter exchange**. The shared substrate's governance perimeter spans more than one Self's home governance perimeter, and the configuration of the spanning is itself substrate content under joint human authority. In Gaia-X and IDS-RAM, governance of perimeter-spanning is encoded at the membership, trust-framework, or contract layer rather than as authored substrate within the exchange. In federated learning, joint authority over aggregation is articulated through orchestrator archetypes and aggregation algorithms. In MPC and TEEs, joint authority is encoded in protocol parameters and attestation. In data trusts and cooperatives, it lives in institutions and contracts. This axis is most load-bearing against cross-organizational neighbors and against the data-space-restatement objection. Gaia-X and IDS-RAM occupy perimeter-spanning territory; the differentiation against them on this axis runs on configuration-as-substrate-content. Claim 1 is architecturally different from Gaia-X and IDS-RAM on the configuration-as-substrate-content move within perimeter-spanning, not Gaia-X or IDS-RAM plus inter-Self extension.

The third axis is **temporary construction with governance-configured persistence versus persistent shared infrastructure as default**. The shared substrate is constructed for an interaction and dissolves on completion, with persistence governance-configured per event from nothing retained to the full substrate as durable record. The dominant pattern in adjacent territories treats shared coordination state either as persistent shared infrastructure (Gaia-X federation services, IDS connectors, Solid pods) or as session-bounded with ad-hoc external persistence (AutoGen, CrewAI). LangGraph's durable execution comes closest as runtime feature, but persistence is treated as transport concern rather than substrate content per event. This axis is most load-bearing against the design-choice objection — persistent shared infrastructure is one configuration option (full retention) within the architecture, not a shape the architecture is forced into.

The conjunction carries the architectural contribution. None of the axes alone suffices: Axis 1 without Axis 2 is Paper 2's intra-Self territory, where substrate-as-content holds within a single Self's perimeter without crossing into joint authority across perimeters; Axis 2 without Axis 1 is the Gaia-X / IDS-RAM pattern, where perimeter-spanning under federated governance holds without the substrate-content commitment the exchange medium itself would carry; Axis 3 without Axes 1 and 2 is the AutoGen / CrewAI pattern, where session-bounded coordination state holds without either substrate-content or perimeter-spanning commitments. ## 5.2 The named foil — opaque inter-agent merge and the merge-mechanism gap

The foil at the merge-mechanism level sharpens §4.2's structural assessment to the specific question of what happens when two or more Selves' outputs need to combine. In contemporary multi-agent and inter-agent-protocol systems, merge of cross-agent content is conducted through arbiter agents that select among candidate outputs, ensemble voters that aggregate at output-space without combining intermediate state, pipeline aggregators that concatenate or transform without preserving contributor distinction, or framework-API-register reducers that operate at the typed-state-field level within one runtime. The merge logic and the intermediate state across which merge occurs are not first-class human-governable substrate content; governance is applied as a wrapper around an opaque or semi-opaque merge, and conflicts arising during merge are transient artifacts to be eliminated for the merge to complete rather than first-class state the merged result carries forward.

The foil sharpens at the merge-mechanism level in three ways §4.2's foil articulation does not develop. First, the unit of merge in contemporary systems is heterogeneous and small-grained: typed state fields, message lists, tool-call payloads, parameter vectors, schema elements, entity-edge assertions. None of these units preserves the structural arrangement of cells with DNA and action layers that Paper 2 establishes within a Self. Second, the merge defaults are themselves heterogeneous and architecturally consequential: last-writer-wins per state key, append-to-transcript, coordinate-wise averaging, no-merge-on-partial-alignment, route-without-mixing. The defaults carry implicit commitments about what survives a merge and what is erased; absent first-class substrate, those commitments are not inspectable as substrate content. Third, governance over merge — when it is articulated at all — operates at framework configuration, contract, or protocol-parameter register rather than as authored substrate content within the merge itself. The merge logic is not substrate; the conflicts surfaced during merge are not substrate; the configuration of the merge is not substrate.

What matters for Claim 2 is that these are properties of the architecture class at the merge-mechanism level, not capability gaps that better LLMs or richer protocols would close. Sharper LLMs and richer protocols would absorb the substrate Claim 2 commits to; the foil literature at §4.2 denies that reading at the substrate-content level, and the same denial holds at the merge-mechanism level. The appropriate response is therefore architectural at the merge-mechanism level specifically. FAI's alternative is substrate-mediated, aspect-level merge with the pattern variant selected by governance-configured orchestration: the merge logic itself is authored substrate content under joint human authority per §4's perimeter-spanning property; conflicts surfaced during merge are first-class addressable substrate state by inheritance from Paper 1 carrying through §4; the pattern-variant configuration is itself substrate content with recursive applicability §8 will develop.

## 10.1 The opening FAI event

Two CKS-governed Selves, each constituted per Paper 2's three-level structure with cells composing into aspects under unified human governance within a Self, with DNA and action layers within every cell, and with instinct and reasoning composing under the instinct/reasoning separation, exist within distinct organizations under distinct authority structures.
