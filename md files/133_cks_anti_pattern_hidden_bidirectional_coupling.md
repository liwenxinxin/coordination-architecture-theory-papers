## Attribution

This work derives from and formalizes the Coordination Knowledge Substrate (CKS) pattern introduced in *Coordination Outside the Model: A Human-Governed Substrate Pattern for AI Systems* (Li, April 2026). It does not introduce new axioms. Its sole contribution is to formalize, as a standalone anti-pattern, the deployment configuration in which substrate and adjacent components couple bidirectionally through hidden mechanisms — a configuration the source paper preempts at §4.5 and that is named compactly within the hybrid-systems-composition decomposition as one of three composition anti-patterns. The present note states the anti-pattern's four operational components, identifies the foundational commitments simultaneously violated, traces the failure mode, specifies the architectural correction, and provides an operational test, so that downstream work can name, recognize, and avoid the anti-pattern without ambiguity.

## Abstract

The CKS pattern admits exactly three legitimate compositions between a substrate and an adjacent AI or data component: cell-to-adjacent consultation, substrate-derived view, and separate concern. All three are unidirectional in their coordination flow. *Hidden bidirectional coupling* is the failure mode in which a deployment's substrate and an adjacent component — a vector index, a customer record system, a search index, a model whose outputs feed back into substrate, a cache layer, a federated database, an integration platform, an ORM-mediated state store — propagate changes to one another through implicit mechanisms (auto-sync, eventual-consistency replication, change-data-capture pipelines, two-way sync integrations, federated bidirectional replication, ORM-level bidirectional propagation, retraining feedback loops) without each direction being a cell-mediated operation under orchestration rules. The configuration violates three foundational architectural commitments simultaneously: the substrate-cell boundary, path retraceability, and hybrid-systems composition. This note formalizes the configuration as a standalone anti-pattern, specifies the architectural correction that decomposes it into separate unidirectional cell-mediated flows, and supplies an operational test of three sharpening properties.

## 1. Why a precise standalone formalization is needed

The hybrid-systems-composition commitment of the CKS pattern names three legitimate ways an adjacent component may compose with a substrate, and it preempts three corresponding anti-patterns. *Hidden bidirectional coupling* is the second of those three. Its standalone formalization matters because the configuration is an architectural failure of three foundational commitments at once — the substrate-cell boundary, path retraceability, and hybrid-systems composition — and because the configuration is operationally common in 2024–2026 deployments that adopt integration platforms, federated database technologies, retrieval indexes with auto-update, ML retraining feedback loops, ORM-level bidirectional state, "lakehouse" architectures, and similar bidirectional infrastructure without recognizing the architectural consequence.

The anti-pattern is also commonly mis-classified: deployments add a reverse direction to a legitimate unidirectional pattern through an integration platform's two-way sync feature or a database's bidirectional replication and read the result as still the legitimate pattern. It is not; the reverse direction is what the anti-pattern names. The standalone treatment provides specificity — four operational components, three sharpening properties, a one-sentence test, an architectural correction — that the broader composition decomposition is not the place to provide; the duplication is intentional for defensive-publication purposes against patentable derivations focused on AI-driven bidirectional integration, federated AI architectures, and ML feedback systems.

## 2. The anti-pattern: four operational components

A deployment exhibits hidden bidirectional coupling to the extent that the following four operational components are present.

**Component 1 — Substrate-to-adjacent automatic propagation outside cell mediation.** Mechanisms exist that propagate substrate changes to adjacent components automatically — change-data-capture pipelines, sync triggers, replication processes, auto-update workflows — without each propagation being a cell-mediated operation under orchestration rules. The flow direction is substrate-to-adjacent; the propagation is automatic and outside the cell architecture.

**Component 2 — Adjacent-to-substrate automatic propagation outside cell mediation.** Mechanisms exist that propagate adjacent-component changes to substrate automatically — reverse sync, replication, push-update workflows — without each propagation being a cell-mediated operation. The flow direction is adjacent-to-substrate; the propagation is automatic and outside the cell architecture.

**Component 3 — Sync mechanisms operating without rule authorization.** The bidirectional sync mechanisms operate without orchestration rules governing them. No rule specifies which direction has authority, when sync occurs, what conflict resolution applies between directions, or what provenance is recorded. Rule authoring as the architectural governance moment is bypassed at the sync layer.

**Component 4 — Conflict resolution between directions outside cell-mediated rule processing.** When the two directions produce conflicting state, conflict resolution occurs through implicit mechanisms — last-write-wins, vector clocks, CRDTs, eventual-consistency convergence, manual reconciliation — rather than through the architectural pattern of substrate-level preservation plus cell-level resolution under rules.

The four components together define the anti-pattern architecturally. A deployment exhibiting any one of the four partially exhibits the anti-pattern; a deployment exhibiting all four exhibits it fully. The qualifier *hidden* is what distinguishes the anti-pattern from explicit bidirectional patterns in which each direction is separately cell-mediated under rules and so does not constitute the failure: it is the implicit, automatic, sync-or-replication-driven character of the propagation that makes the configuration architecturally inadmissible.

## 3. Which CKS commitments are violated

Hidden bidirectional coupling is distinctive in violating three foundational commitments simultaneously through the same operational mechanism.

**The substrate-cell boundary is directly violated.** The architectural separation between substrate (state) and cells (behavior) commits to a boundary across which only cell-mediated operations propagate. Hidden bidirectional sync creates an effective third architectural position — the bidirectional sync mechanism — that bridges substrate and adjacent components and so spans the boundary outside cell mediation. The substrate-cell-boundary decomposition's specific commitments to what crosses the boundary, what holds state, and how cell-to-cell communication routes through substrate are each compromised at sync moments.

**Path retraceability is directly violated.** The architectural commitment to retraceable substrate state changes through six provenance metadata fields fails when bidirectional propagation produces substrate changes whose origin, authority, rule, and timing are not addressable. Hidden propagation typically records partial provenance (or none); the path-retraceability decomposition's accountability vocabulary is not satisfied at bidirectional-coupling moments.

**Hybrid-systems composition is directly violated.** The architectural commitment to three legitimate composition patterns — all unidirectional in their coordination flow — fails when bidirectional coupling adds the reverse direction outside the legitimate pattern's architecture. Cell-to-adjacent consultation is unidirectional cell-to-adjacent; bidirectional coupling adds an adjacent-to-substrate direction outside the cell's processing. Substrate-derived view is unidirectional substrate-to-adjacent and non-authoritative; bidirectional coupling adds an adjacent-to-substrate direction that violates both the directionality and the non-authoritativeness. The integrating frame for hybrid composition is the frame the anti-pattern operates outside; the composition anti-pattern named compactly within the decomposition is precisely this case.

The cascade implications follow from the same mechanism. Rule authoring as the governance moment is bypassed systematically. The AI-as-substrate-mediator commitment and its property that LLM writes occur under orchestration rules are extended-implicated when AI components participate in bidirectional flows. The determinism contract is extended-implicated through sync-timing, conflict-resolution, and eventual-consistency non-determinism, with the read-determinism guarantee extended-violated when reads of substrate may return different results depending on whether sync has propagated. The human-governed commitment is extended-implicated, because humans cannot govern hidden bidirectional flows through the architectural rule-mediation mechanism. The composition-requirements requirement that AI mediation operate at every layer, and the conflict-as-first-class commitment with its cell-level-resolution-under-rules counterpart, are similarly extended-implicated.

## 4. The failure mode

The downstream consequences are operationally specific. Substrate state becomes emergent from interleaving flows: rule-mediated cell writes interleave with bidirectional propagation, and the resulting state may not match what any orchestration rule specified. Humans inspecting substrate cannot identify change provenance — they see content but cannot determine which changes came from rule-governed cells and which from bidirectional sync, and so cannot answer the four accountability questions the path-retraceability decomposition supports. Conflicts resolve outside the architectural pattern: rather than substrate-level preservation plus cell-level resolution under rules, conflicts are resolved implicitly through last-write-wins, vector clocks, CRDTs, or eventual-consistency convergence, so the architectural commitment fails not by leaving conflicts unresolved but by resolving them outside the architecture.

Recovery is operationally constrained: when problematic state arises, recovery requires unwinding bidirectional flows across both substrate and adjacent components, and the operational complexity may make recovery infeasible in the time window during which it would matter. Debugging is operationally difficult, because bidirectional flows make it ambiguous which direction caused a specific state. Sync timing introduces non-determinism: substrate reads may return different results depending on whether the most recent sync has propagated, in a way unidirectional patterns do not exhibit. Eventual consistency produces temporary divergence — substrate has one state, the adjacent component has another, sync pending — and operations during the divergence window produce inconsistent results. Where the adjacent component is a model retrained on substrate output and whose outputs flow back into substrate as input, the bidirectional flow may amplify model biases or produce runaway feedback that no orchestration rule sanctioned.

The failure mode also compounds with sibling anti-patterns. The adjacent-to-substrate direction of a bidirectional flow typically itself constitutes the one-direction ungoverned-writer anti-pattern; the bidirectional changes typically lack full provenance and so produce instances of the non-addressable-writes anti-pattern. Where the adjacent component is an LLM-resident store treated as authoritative, or an external tool treated as authoritative, the related anti-patterns compound further.

## 5. The architectural correction

The architectural correction operates through three foundational commitments together. *Unidirectional flows under hybrid-systems composition*: every flow between substrate and an adjacent component must be unidirectional, with substrate-to-adjacent flows occupying the substrate-derived-view position (read-only relative to substrate) and adjacent-to-substrate flows occupying the cell-to-adjacent-consultation position (a cell consults the adjacent component, processes the response, and writes substrate under rules); no flow operates in both directions through the same mechanism. *Cell-mediation for all flows*: each unidirectional flow is cell-mediated, with derived-view generation expressed as rule-authorized cell operations or as substrate-resident derivation rules, and with adjacent-to-substrate flows occurring through cells that consult the adjacent component and write substrate under orchestration rules with provenance recorded. *Substrate-cell boundary preservation*: adjacent components do not span the boundary; they occupy clearly-defined positions as consultation targets, derived-view destinations, or separate concerns, and the third architectural position that hidden bidirectional sync mechanisms occupied is eliminated.

The correction further requires that bidirectional patterns be decomposed into separate unidirectional operations. A two-way customer-record sync becomes a consultation (cells consult the record system, write substrate under rules) plus a derived view (substrate-derived views supplied to the record system through cell-mediated push). A bidirectional retrieval index becomes a derived view plus a consultation. A federated database becomes substrate-as-authoritative with other databases occupying derived-view or separate-concern positions. An ML feedback loop becomes an explicit consultation with model retraining as a separate concern that does not couple back into substrate automatically. Each unidirectional cell-mediated flow records full provenance; conflict resolution is routed through the architectural conflict-handling pattern; auto-sync, change-data-capture pipelines, eventual-consistency replication, and similar implicit mechanisms are replaced with explicit cell invocations, possibly triggered on schedule or on event by an orchestration-layer workflow but each invocation rule-authorized.

The correction is operationally consequential for deployments with extensive bidirectional sync infrastructure: it requires re-architecting integration patterns, retrieval pipelines, federated database topologies, and feedback loops to occupy unidirectional cell-mediated positions. The framing that "automatic sync just works" is replaced with the architectural commitment that *just works* must mean *operates within the unidirectional cell-mediated architecture*; bidirectional auto-sync that bypasses the architecture fails the architectural commitments even when it produces operational results.

## 6. What hidden bidirectional coupling is NOT

The anti-pattern is defined precisely; it is equally important to state what it is not, because each of the following neighbors is a real and reasonable configuration in some other deployment.

*Not unidirectional cell-to-adjacent consultation.* A cell consulting an adjacent component, processing the response, and writing substrate under rules is the legitimate consultation pattern. The anti-pattern arises specifically when the adjacent-component side then propagates back into substrate through a hidden mechanism; consultation alone does not constitute the anti-pattern.

*Not unidirectional substrate-derived view.* A read-only adjacent component receiving substrate-derived state — a search index, a vector index, a downstream report — is the legitimate derived-view pattern. The anti-pattern arises specifically when the adjacent component writes back into substrate; derived view alone does not constitute the anti-pattern.

*Not explicit bidirectional patterns where each direction is separately cell-mediated.* A deployment that genuinely needs data exchange in both directions can satisfy the architecture by decomposing the exchange into separate unidirectional cell-mediated flows — a cell-mediated push and a cell-mediated pull, each with its own rule, provenance, and conflict semantics. Such an arrangement is bidirectional in net data flow but not in mechanism, and so is not the anti-pattern.

*Not separate-concern composition.* An adjacent system used for non-coordination concerns that does not couple to substrate at all is the legitimate separate-concern position. The anti-pattern is specifically about coupling, and specifically about coupling that is bidirectional and hidden.

The architectural distinction is precise: legitimate consultation is cell-to-adjacent and the adjacent component does not flow back to substrate automatically; legitimate derived view is substrate-to-adjacent and the adjacent component does not write back; legitimate explicit bidirectional exchange decomposes into separate cell-mediated unidirectional flows; the anti-pattern is the configuration in which both directions exist and at least one is hidden.

## 7. Operational test

A deployment exhibits hidden bidirectional coupling if any of the four operational components from §2 are present at any time during the deployment's existence, and if any of the following three sharpening properties also hold.

**Property 1 — Flow-directionality.** Every flow between substrate and an adjacent component is unidirectional, examined at the level of mechanism rather than at the level of net data movement. The test passes if no mechanism propagates state in both directions; it fails if any single mechanism (a sync process, a replication topology, a CDC pipeline, an integration-platform connector, an ORM bidirectional binding) propagates state in both directions, regardless of how rare the reverse direction is in operation.

**Property 2 — Sync-mechanism cell-mediation.** Every mechanism that propagates state between substrate and an adjacent component operates through cell-mediated processing under orchestration rules. The test passes if every such propagation is invokable as a cell operation with a corresponding rule and recorded provenance; it fails if any propagation occurs through auto-sync, eventual-consistency replication, CDC pipeline, retraining feedback, or similar mechanism outside cell-mediation.

**Property 3 — Bidirectional-conflict-resolution-rule.** Where two directions could produce conflicting state, resolution occurs through cell-mediated rule processing rather than through implicit convergence mechanisms. The test passes if a deliberately-injected bidirectional conflict triggers a cell-mediated resolution path with a rule that names the resolution; it fails if the conflict resolves through last-write-wins, vector-clock convergence, CRDT merge, eventual consistency, or unstructured manual reconciliation.

**One-sentence test.** If a deployment has substrate and an adjacent component — a retrieval index, a vector database, an external customer-record system, a search index, a model, a cache layer, a federated database, an external SaaS system, an ORM-mediated state store, an integration platform — coupling bidirectionally through any hidden mechanism (auto-sync, eventual-consistency replication, change-data-capture pipeline, two-way sync integration, federated bidirectional replication, ORM-level bidirectional propagation, retraining feedback loop) without each direction being a cell-mediated operation under orchestration rules, the deployment exhibits hidden bidirectional coupling and the substrate-cell boundary, path retraceability, and hybrid-systems composition commitments fail simultaneously.

## 8. Conclusion

Hidden bidirectional coupling is the configuration in which substrate and an adjacent component propagate changes to one another through implicit mechanisms outside cell-mediation. The configuration is operationally common in 2024–2026 deployments because integration platforms, federated databases, retrieval indexes with auto-update, ML feedback loops, and "lakehouse" architectures all routinely support bidirectional patterns; the configuration is architecturally inadmissible because it violates the substrate-cell boundary, path retraceability, and hybrid-systems composition commitments through the same operational mechanism.

The architectural correction is to decompose every bidirectional coupling into separate unidirectional cell-mediated flows, each with its own rule, provenance, and conflict semantics — substrate-to-adjacent flows occupying the substrate-derived-view position, adjacent-to-substrate flows occupying the cell-to-adjacent-consultation position, and explicit data exchange that genuinely needs both directions decomposing into two unidirectional cell-mediated mechanisms.

The configuration is the second of three composition-layer anti-patterns, sitting between the one-direction case (ungoverned writes from an adjacent component into substrate) and the substrate-substitute case (an adjacent component functionally replacing substrate). The directionality-of-flow lens organizes the sub-cluster: one-direction violations of the boundary, two-direction violations of the boundary, and substitution violations of the substrate's role. Naming the second case as standalone gives downstream readers a precise specification of the failure mode and the path back to architectural compliance, distinct from what the broader composition decomposition can be expected to provide.
